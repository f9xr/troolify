Paper Size Converter: Exact mm, Inches & Pixel Dimensions

Designers, printers and document builders constantly juggle three unit systems for the same sheet of paper. A4 is "210 by 297" in millimetres, "8.27 by 11.69" in inches, and "2480 by 3508" or "794 by 1123" in pixels depending on DPI. This converter produces all of them at once, for A, B and US series.

Understanding the Paper Series

The ISO A series is the global standard: each size down (A4 to A5) is exactly half the area of the previous one, with the same proportions. The B series sits between A sizes and is common for books and envelopes. US sizes (Letter, Legal, Tabloid) use imperial inches and are dominant in North America - a frequent source of conversion confusion.

How to Use the Converter

  1. Pick a series - A (ISO), B (ISO) or US sizes.
  2. Choose the size - A4, A5, B5, Letter, and so on.
  3. Set the DPI - 72 or 96 for screen work, 300 for print, or enter a custom value.
  4. Flip the orientation if you need landscape instead of portrait.

The Pixel Math, Explained

Pixels are the only unit that depends on output. First convert millimetres to inches (divide by 25.4), then multiply by the DPI: pixels = mm ÷ 25.4 × DPI. That is why the same A4 sheet is 794 × 1123 px at 96 DPI but 2480 × 3508 px at 300 DPI - the paper never changes, only the resolution it is captured at.

Real-World Uses

  • Print designers set up A4 canvases at 300 DPI for sharp physical output.
  • Web developers export PDFs sized for exact on-screen dimensions at 96 DPI.
  • Anyone mixing US Letter and A4 files avoids the shrink-and-stretch surprise at the printer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the pixel size of A4 paper?

A4 is 210 x 297 mm. At 300 DPI that makes 2480 x 3508 pixels; at 96 DPI it is 794 x 1123 pixels. Exact pixel dimensions always depend on the DPI you choose.

How do I convert millimetres to pixels?

Divide the millimetre value by 25.4 to get inches, then multiply by the DPI. So 210 mm at 300 DPI is (210 / 25.4) x 300 = 2480 pixels.

What DPI should I use for print vs screen?

Use 300 DPI (or 600 DPI for fine detail) when preparing for print. Screens typically assume 72 or 96 DPI, so a design sized for print will appear much larger in pixels than the equivalent on-screen document.
