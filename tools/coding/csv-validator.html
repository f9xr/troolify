CSV Validator: Two-Second Check Before Your ETL Runs

A single unquoted comma can silently shift every column in a CSV - and by the time the data lands in your database, the damage is done. The Troolify CSV Validator hunts for column drift, unterminated quoted cells, stray quotes and empty rows, and reports each one with its line number and severity before you load a single row.

What the CSV Validator Does

The validator parses the file as a state machine following RFC 4180: quoted fields stretch across newlines, doubled quotes represent escaped quotes, and delimiters inside quotes are preserved. Every data row is then compared against the header - if the field count differs, that row is flagged as column drift.

How to Use the CSV Validator

  1. Paste or type your CSV - or load one of the two sample datasets.
  2. Pick the delimiter - comma, tab or semicolon.
  3. Click Validate - or let it auto-validate as you type.
  4. Review the issues - errors are red, warnings are yellow.

Use Cases

Data engineers check exports before running ETL jobs. QA teams validate CSV fixtures generated by automation. Support staff inspect CSV dumps emailed from production. Anyone who has ever imported a "clean" export that turned out to be anything but will appreciate the line-level warning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the CSV Validator check for?

It checks column drift (rows with fewer or more fields than the header), unterminated quoted fields, stray or unclosed quotes, empty rows, and trailing commas. It reports the line number of each issue along with its severity (error or warning).

What CSV delimiters are supported?

You can choose comma, tab or semicolon as the delimiter. The validator is RFC 4180 compliant for comma-delimited files and handles the same quoting rules for all three delimiters.

Is my CSV uploaded?

No. All validation runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded to or stored on any server.

What is column drift?

Column drift is when a data row has a different number of fields than the header. This often means a missing delimiter, an extra comma, or a missing field - and can break spreadsheets, databases and ETL pipelines.

Conclusion

Catch malformed CSV in seconds: column drift, unterminated quotes and stray delimiters all flagged by line number with clear severity - entirely in your browser.
