Cookie Security Analyzer: Audit HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite

Session cookies are the keys to your users' accounts, and a single missing attribute can expose them to theft or cross-site abuse. This cookie security analyzer reads your Set-Cookie headers and scores each cookie on the flags that matter: HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite and the hardened cookie prefixes.

Paste one header or a whole response and review the findings in plain language.

The flags that protect a cookie

Secure keeps the cookie off plain HTTP. HttpOnly hides it from JavaScript, neutralising theft through XSS. SameSite governs whether the cookie rides along on cross-site requests - Lax is a sound default and None must always be paired with Secure. The __Host- and __Secure- name prefixes let the browser enforce additional rules for you.

How the score works

Each cookie starts at 100 and loses points for missing or unsafe attributes, with the largest penalties for exposed session material. The tool reports an average across all cookies and highlights the worst offender, so you can prioritise the fix that matters most.

How to use Cookie Security Analyzer

  1. Copy the Set-Cookie headers from your browser dev tools or an HTTP response.
  2. Paste them one per line into the input.
  3. Read the per-cookie findings and the overall risk.
  4. Add the missing attributes and re-run to confirm the fix.

3. Debugging a broken login

Spot a SameSite=None cookie that is missing Secure and being rejected.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Session cookies should always be HttpOnly and Secure.
  • Prefer SameSite=Lax unless a genuine cross-site flow requires None.
  • Use the __Host- prefix for the most sensitive cookie to lock down path and domain.

Key takeaways

  • HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite are the three core protections.
  • SameSite=None is only valid alongside Secure.
  • A low score points directly at the attribute that needs attention.

Frequently asked questions

What is HttpOnly?

An attribute that stops client-side JavaScript from reading the cookie, which blocks the most common session-theft attack.

Do I always need SameSite=None?

Only when the cookie must be sent on genuine cross-site requests, such as an embedded widget. It must then also be Secure.

Is this a live scan?

No. It analyses the header text you paste; it does not contact your server or test cookies at runtime.

What do the __Host- and __Secure- prefixes do?

They instruct the browser to reject the cookie unless its security requirements are met, adding defence in depth.

Is my data uploaded?

No. Everything is analysed in your browser.
