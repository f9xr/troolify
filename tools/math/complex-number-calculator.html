Complex Number Calculator: Arithmetic With an Imaginary Part

A complex number pairs a real part with an imaginary part written using i, where i is the square root of -1. Because i² = -1, the usual rules of arithmetic still work with one addition to the multiplication table, and the square root of a negative number stops being an error. This calculator performs that arithmetic and then converts the result to polar form.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Pick the operation - add, subtract, multiply or divide.
  2. Enter both numbers as real part plus imaginary part. Negative imaginary parts are fine.
  3. Read the answer in rectangular form, then in polar form with modulus and argument.

Multiplying: Watch the i² Term

Multiply the real parts, the imaginary parts, and the two cross terms separately, then simplify. The cross terms are where it goes wrong by hand: two imaginary parts multiplied together give a negative real number, because i² = -1. The result always comes back in the form real + imaginary i.

Dividing: Use the Conjugate

Dividing complex numbers looks impossible until you multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator, which flips the sign on its imaginary part. Squaring (c + di) then gives c² - d², a real number, so the division is finished with ordinary arithmetic. When both parts of the divisor are zero the result is undefined, and this calculator says so rather than showing a nonsense figure.

Rectangular and Polar Form

Every complex number can be drawn as a point, and that point has a distance and a direction. The modulus is the distance, the square root of a² + b². The argument is the angle in degrees, worked out with the inverse tangent and then adjusted to land in the right quadrant. Polar form, r(cosθ + i·sinθ), makes multiplication and division much easier because the moduli multiply and the arguments add.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you add and subtract complex numbers?

Add the real parts together and the imaginary parts together, keeping the i in place. So (3 + 2i) + (1 - 5i) is 4 - 3i, because 3 + 1 = 4 and 2 - 5 = -3.

How do you divide complex numbers?

Multiply the first number by the conjugate of the second, then divide by the sum of the squares of the second number’s parts. The denominator becomes real, which is the whole trick that makes the division work out cleanly.

What is the modulus and argument?

The modulus is the distance from the origin, found with the Pythagorean theorem: the square root of a² + b². The argument is the angle from the positive real axis, found with the inverse tangent of b divided by a, with the correct quadrant chosen.

Conclusion

Complex numbers are just pairs of real numbers obeying ordinary arithmetic, with one extra rule for division. Once you accept the i in front of the imaginary part is a label rather than a mystery, the whole topic becomes routine. This calculator handles the bookkeeping so you can concentrate on the idea.
