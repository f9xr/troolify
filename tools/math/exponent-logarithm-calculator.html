Exponent and Logarithm Calculator: The Two Sides of One Idea

Exponents let you write large and very small numbers compactly, and logarithms let you undo that writing. Everything from compound interest to pH values to how loud a sound is runs through this one relationship. This calculator evaluates both sides and shows the equivalent statements, so the link stops being abstract.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Pick the operation - a power, an nth root, or a logarithm to any base.
  2. Enter the values. The labels change to match the operation, so there is no guessing which box wants what.
  3. Read the answer with the working, including the rewrite for roots and logs.

Powers and Exponent Rules

  • Multiplying same bases: add the exponents, so 2⁵ × 2⁴ = 2¹⁶.
  • Dividing same bases: subtract the exponents.
  • Power of a power: multiply the exponents, so (2²)⁵ = 2¹⁰.
  • Zero and negative bases: a negative base with a fractional exponent has no real answer, and this calculator says so instead of returning a misleading figure.

Roots Are Powers in Disguise

The square root of a number is that number to the power one half, the cube root is the power one third, and the nth root is the power 1/n. This is why an even root of a negative number is undefined over the reals, while a cube root of a negative number is perfectly ordinary. Fractions with a zero denominator are also undefined.

Logarithms: Ask the Other Question

Writing 2⁵ = 1024 and then asking "what power of 2 gives 1024?" is asking for log base 2 of 1024, and the answer is 10. The base must be positive and not equal to 1, and the value must be greater than zero. The two common bases are base 10 (written log) and base e, written ln or loge. Because the calculator shows the rewritten form, you can always check a log answer by raising the base back to the power you got.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I evaluate a power like 2 to the 10?

Multiply the base by itself as many times as the exponent. 2 to the 10 is 2 multiplied by 2 ten times, which gives 1024. Large results switch automatically to scientific notation so they stay readable.

What is the relationship between exponents and logs?

They undo each other. a to the power b equals c exactly when log base a of c equals b. That is why an exponential growth curve and its logarithm describe the same relationship, and why you can solve either problem by converting to the other.

How do you take the nth root of a number?

The nth root is the value that multiplied by itself n times gives the number, written as the number to the power 1 over n. The 3rd root of 27 is 27 to the power one third, which is 3.
