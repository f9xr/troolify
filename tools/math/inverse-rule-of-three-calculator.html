Inverse Rule of Three Calculator: When More Means Less

In many real-world situations, increasing one quantity causes another to decrease. More workers finish a job faster. Higher speed means shorter travel time. This is inverse proportion, and the inverse rule of three is the quickest way to calculate the unknown value.

How Inverse Proportion Differs

Unlike direct proportion where A/B = C/D, inverse proportion follows A × B = C × D. The product of each pair stays constant. If you triple one value, the other drops to one-third. This tool applies that formula automatically and shows the algebraic steps so you can follow along.

  • Solve for D: D = (A × B) ÷ C
  • Solve for C: C = (A × B) ÷ D
  • Solve for B: B = (C × D) ÷ A
  • Solve for A: A = (C × D) ÷ B

Frequently Asked Questions

How is inverse different from direct proportion?

In direct proportion, both values move in the same direction: if one doubles, so does the other. In inverse proportion, they move in opposite directions: if one doubles, the other halves. The formula changes accordingly - direct uses A/B = C/D while inverse uses A × B = C × D.

When do I use the inverse rule of three?

Use it whenever increasing one quantity causes another to decrease proportionally - for example, more workers mean fewer days to finish a job, higher speed means less travel time, or increased pressure means reduced volume. The product of the paired values stays constant.

Can the same four boxes solve both direct and inverse?

Not in this tool. The direct Rule of Three Calculator assumes A/B = C/D, while this inverse calculator assumes A × B = C × D. They are separate tools because the underlying formula is different, even though the layout looks similar.

What real situations are inverse proportional?

Common examples include workers vs. time to complete a task, speed vs. travel duration for a fixed distance, pipe diameter vs. flow time to fill a tank, and the relationship between pressure and volume in gases (Boyle's Law).
