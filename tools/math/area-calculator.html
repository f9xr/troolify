Area Calculator: Find the Space Inside Any Shape

Whether you are flooring a room, planting a garden bed or solving a geometry assignment, knowing the area of a shape is one of the most common everyday calculations. This tool covers six standard shapes and converts the result into whichever unit you prefer, all inside your browser.

Six Shapes, One Tool

  • Square - enter one side and get the area instantly.
  • Rectangle - enter length and width.
  • Triangle - enter the base and perpendicular height.
  • Circle - enter the radius and pi does the rest.
  • Trapezoid - enter both parallel bases and the height between them.
  • Parallelogram - enter the base and the perpendicular height.

Switch between square metres, square feet, square inches and square centimetres with a single dropdown. The conversion happens instantly so you can compare results across unit systems without retyping.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Pick a shape from the buttons at the top.
  2. Enter the dimensions shown - the inputs update to match the shape you chose.
  3. Read the result in the result card below, or change the unit dropdown to convert.
  4. Copy the result to your clipboard with one click.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the formula for each shape?

Square: side x side. Rectangle: length x width. Triangle: base x height / 2. Circle: pi x radius squared. Trapezoid: (base A + base B) x height / 2. Parallelogram: base x height. All formulas are applied automatically when you enter the dimensions.

Can I switch units after entering values?

Yes. The unit selector converts the result into the chosen unit instantly. Your input values stay the same and only the displayed area changes, so you can compare results in different units without retyping anything.

What about a non-right triangle?

The formula base x height / 2 works for any triangle, not just right-angled ones. The height is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite vertex, regardless of the triangle's angles.

What is a trapezoid?

A trapezoid (or trapezium in British English) is a four-sided shape with exactly one pair of parallel sides, called bases. The area is the average of the two bases multiplied by the perpendicular height between them.
