Quadratic Equation Solver: Both Roots, Every Time

A quadratic is any equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0. It describes a parabola, and solving one means finding the exact points where the curve crosses the x-axis. Because a parabola can cross zero, one or two times, a good solver has to handle all three cases - which is exactly what this one does.

The Quadratic Formula

To solve ax² + bx + c = 0, use x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a. The plus and minus give two roots, or one repeated root when they coincide. This works for every quadratic with a ≠ 0, including ones that cannot be factored with whole numbers - which is where manual methods tend to stall.

How to Use This Solver

  1. Enter a, b and c from your equation. Watch the signs: b = -3 means b is negative.
  2. Choose your rounding so decimal roots display the way your course expects.
  3. Read the roots plus the discriminant, the vertex and the sum and product of roots as a cross-check.

Reading the Discriminant

The expression b² - 4ac decides the shape of the answer before you calculate anything. A positive discriminant means two real roots, so the parabola cuts the x-axis twice. Zero means the curve just touches the axis at one point, its turning point. Negative means no real roots at all - the parabola sits entirely above or below the axis, and any solutions are complex, with a real part and an imaginary part.

Two Handy Checks

  • Sum of roots equals -b/a. If your two answers do not add to that, an arithmetic slip has happened.
  • Product of roots equals c/a. Checking both at once catches almost every error before you hand the work in.

Finding the Vertex

Every parabola has a turning point at x = -b/2a. Substituting that back in gives the y-coordinate. The vertex is the lowest or highest point of the curve, which is often what a question actually asks for when it mentions optimisation or a maximum profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the quadratic formula?

The quadratic formula is x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a. It finds the values of x that make ax² + bx + c equal to zero, and it works for every quadratic where a is not zero.

What does the discriminant tell me?

The discriminant is the part b² - 4ac. If it is positive you have two different real roots, if it is zero the parabola touches the axis at a single repeated root, and if it is negative the roots are complex, so the curve never crosses the x-axis.

What happens if a is zero?

The equation is no longer quadratic - it becomes bx + c = 0, a straight line with the single solution x = -c/b. This calculator flags that case instead of dividing by zero.

Are my equations saved or sent anywhere?

No. Everything is computed in your browser tab. Your coefficients are never uploaded or stored, so you can solve coursework privately.

Conclusion

The quadratic formula looks heavy but it is just a shortcut for factoring that works even when factoring does not. Practise a few until the discriminant feels like a diagnostic rather than a mystery, and parabolas stop being intimidating.
