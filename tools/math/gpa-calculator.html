GPA Calculator: Turn Your Grades Into One Clear Number

Every school turns a messy transcript full of letters and credits into a single number: your grade point average. That number decides course eligibility, scholarships, honours lists and sometimes graduation itself. This calculator does the arithmetic correctly, including weighting, so you always know where you actually stand.

What Is a GPA?

A GPA is the average of the grade points you earn across your courses, weighted by how many credits each course is worth. A 4.0 scale turns an A into 4.0 quality points and an F into 0.0. Multiply each course's points by its credits, sum them, and divide by total credits. Courses carrying more credits move the number more - which is exactly the point of weighting.

How to Use This GPA Calculator

  1. Choose your scale - letter grades on the standard 4.0 scale, or percentage grades that get converted for you.
  2. Add a row per course with its credit hours and grade. Use "Add course" as many times as you need.
  3. Enter past results in the previous GPA and credits boxes if you want a cumulative figure rather than a term-only average.
  4. Read the result - weighted GPA, total quality points, total credits and the letter grade that GPA represents.

Weighted vs Unweighted: Which One You Need

Most US high schools report an unweighted GPA where an A in an honours course counts the same as an A in standard coursework. Colleges that care about rigour then recalculate a weighted GPA, often adding extra points for AP, IB and honours classes. Use the weighted figure when a school publishes a weighting scale, and the unweighted row in the results when applying to schools that want to see your GPA "as is". Entering everything on one scale keeps your transcript internally consistent.

Why Cumulative GPA Matters

A term GPA can swing wildly on a single bad week, but almost nothing important uses it. Scholarships, graduate admissions and Dean's List all read your cumulative GPA, which blends every credit you have ever earned. Because your old credits are already baked into your old GPA, you convert it back to quality points before adding the new term - that conversion is the step most people get wrong, and the one this calculator performs for you.

Common Grade Point Scales

  • 4.0 scale (most US schools): A+ to A = 4.0, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0.0.
  • Percentage mode: each mark is converted linearly as percentage ÷ 25, so 100% = 4.0 and 75% = 3.0.
  • Weighted scales: some districts add 0.5 or 1.0 to AP, IB and honours grades before the average is taken. Apply that bonus to the marks you enter, since this calculator follows the standard scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a weighted GPA calculated?

Multiply each course grade point by its credit hours, add the results together, then divide by the total credit hours. Weighting means a 4-credit course counts twice as much as a 2-credit course, which is why the total is not just the average of your grade letters.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

A weighted GPA credits each course according to its credit hours. An unweighted GPA treats every course as equal regardless of credits. Students taking honours, AP or IB courses often keep an unweighted GPA for applications and a weighted GPA for their transcript, so schools frequently report both.

How do I calculate my cumulative GPA?

Convert your existing GPA to quality points by multiplying it by the credits earned so far, add the quality points from this term, then divide by the total credits across both periods. Enter your previous GPA and credits in the calculator and it does this for you.

Is my grade data sent anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs inside your browser tab and your course names and grades are never uploaded, stored or transmitted. You can safely enter real marks on a shared or public computer.

Conclusion

Your GPA is a snapshot, not a verdict. Use it to see which subjects are pulling the average down, then target those. Run the numbers again after every assessment block and you will know exactly where you stand before the transcript does.
