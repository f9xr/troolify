Triangle Solver: Find Any Missing Side, Angle or Area

A triangle is fully described by three independent measurements - three sides, two sides and an included angle, or one side and two angles. Give this solver whichever three you have and it returns the complete picture: all three sides, all three angles, the area, the perimeter and how the shape is classified.

How to Use the Triangle Solver

  1. Choose what you know - three sides, two sides plus the included angle, or one side plus two angles.
  2. Enter the values. Angles are in degrees.
  3. Read the full result - missing lengths, all angles, area, perimeter and the triangle type.

The Two Laws That Do The Work

  • Law of cosines: c² = a² + b² - 2ab·cos(C). Use it to find a side from the other two sides and the included angle, or to find an angle from all three sides.
  • Law of sines: a / sin(A) = b / sin(B) = c / sin(C). Use it when you know one side and want another side, or to find a missing angle.

Area and Perimeter

With a base and its perpendicular height the area is simply half of base times height. With only the three sides, Heron's formula does the job: work out the semi-perimeter s, then take the square root of s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c). Perimeter is just a + b + c, and the semi-perimeter is half of that.

Types of Triangle

  • By sides: scalene (all different), isosceles (two equal), equilateral (all three equal).
  • By angles: acute (all under 90°), right (one 90°), obtuse (one over 90°).
  • Valid triangles only exist when the longest side is strictly less than the sum of the other two. This calculator checks that inequality, which is the most common reason a set of measurements cannot be a triangle at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you find a missing side of a triangle?

With two sides and the included angle use the law of cosines, c² = a² + b² - 2ab·cos(C). With one side and two angles use the law of sines, a/sin(A) = b/sin(B) = c/sin(C), and solve for the unknown side.

What does the law of cosines do?

It relates the sides of any triangle to one of its angles, and it works on right triangles too where it reproduces the Pythagorean theorem. It is the tool for finding a side when you know the other two sides and the angle between them.

How is the area of a triangle calculated?

If you know the base and height, the area is half of base times height. If you only know the three sides, use Heron's formula: area = √(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)) where s is the semi-perimeter, half the total perimeter.

Is my triangle data uploaded?

No. All calculations run inside your browser tab. Your measurements are never transmitted, stored or logged.

Conclusion

Triangles are the base of trigonometry, surveying and graphics, and the two laws involved are worth memorising. Sides alone means cosine rule, an angle plus two sides means cosine rule then sine rule. Recognising which situation you are in is the real skill, and this tool lets you check that instinct instantly.
