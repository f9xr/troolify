Radians to Degrees Converter: Two Units for the Same Angle

Angles live a double life. The everyday world thinks in degrees - a right angle is 90 and a full circle is 360. Mathematics and programming think in radians, where a full circle is exactly two pi. This converter translates between the two instantly, so you never have to pause a calculation or a codebase to look up the formula by hand.

What Exactly Is a Radian?

A radian is the angle you get when you wrap a length of arc equal to the radius of a circle around the centre. Because the whole circumference is two pi radii, a full circle is exactly two pi radians. The radian ties angular measure directly to the geometry of the circle rather than splitting it into an arbitrary 360 slices, which is why it appears naturally in every formula involving rotation, arc length or wave motion.

The Simple Formula

The conversion is just one multiplication either way: multiply degrees by pi and divide by 180 to get radians, or multiply radians by 180 and divide by pi to get degrees. Both directions are exact; any rounding happens only because you choose how many decimal places you want to keep.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is a radian?

A radian is the angle swept out when you walk a distance along the arc of a circle equal to the circle's radius. Because the whole circumference is two pi radii, a full circle is exactly 2 pi radians. The radian ties angle measure directly to the geometry of the circle rather than dividing it into an arbitrary 360 slices.

Why is 180 degrees equal to pi radians?

Half a circle is 180 degrees, and the circumference of a full circle is two pi times its radius. Half of that arc length is pi radii, which is the definition of pi radians. So 180 degrees and pi radians are just two ways of describing the same angle, which is why degrees times pi divided by 180 gives the equivalent in radians.

Can I type mathematical expressions with pi in the radians field?

Yes. The radians field accepts expressions like pi/2, 3*pi/4, pi, or 2*pi. Type the word pi and the converter evaluates it as the mathematical constant. You can use multiplication (2*pi), division (pi/4) and parentheses in any valid combination. Plain decimal numbers like 1.5708 work as well.

Why do programmers and mathematicians prefer radians?

Radians link angle measure directly to arc length and the geometry of circles. This makes calculus formulas, trigonometric derivatives and series expansions much cleaner: for example, the derivative of sin(x) is simply cos(x) when x is in radians, but would need an awkward scaling factor in degrees. That is why virtually all programming languages, physics equations and engineering formulas use radians by default.
