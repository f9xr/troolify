Citation Generator: Correct References in Four Styles

Every style guide is answering the same four questions - who wrote it, when, what is it called, and where can I find it - in a different fixed order. Once you see that pattern, the formats stop being mysterious. This generator renders the same source in APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago and Harvard so you can match your department's requirements exactly.

How to Use the Citation Generator

  1. Choose the style your course or publisher requires.
  2. Pick the source type - book, journal article or website page - and the relevant fields appear.
  3. Fill in the details and copy both the reference entry and the in-text citation.

How the Four Styles Differ

  • APA 7 uses an author-date system: the reference lists the year in brackets after the author, and the in-text citation is (Smith, 2021).
  • MLA 9 leans on containers. The core is the author and title, and the in-text citation gives a page number where one exists.
  • Chicago is the most flexible, and offers both notes-bibliography and author-date systems. The bibliography entry repeats the author name the first time.
  • Harvard resembles APA but is used more heavily in the UK, Europe and Australia, with the year also given by name in the text.

Handling Several Authors

The rules differ more than students expect. APA lists up to twenty authors, using a comma before the final name and an ampersand. MLA names the first author then "et al." from the third. Chicago and Harvard both truncate to the first author followed by "et al." once there are more than three. Getting the author list wrong is one of the most common reasons a reference list loses marks, so check that line especially.

Checking Your Work

After you paste the reference into your document, read it back as a stranger would. Is there enough to find the source without guessing? Is the capitalisation right, with only the first word and proper nouns capitalised in APA and MLA titles? Are dates, volume and issue numbers present? A reference that fails any of those tests usually needs a second pass. For word counts and timing, our Reading Time Calculator will tell you how long your finished draft takes to read.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which citation style should I use?

Follow whatever your department or course specifies, because they rarely accept a substitute. This calculator offers APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago and Harvard so you can see the same source rendered four ways and see how each answers the same questions differently.

How do I cite a website with no author?

When there is no named author, many styles use the organisation name, and failing that the page title in the author position. Include the site name, the full URL and the date you accessed it, since web pages change without warning.

What is the difference between a reference list and a bibliography?

A reference list contains only the works you actually cited. A bibliography also includes works you consulted but did not cite. MLA and Chicago call it a Works Cited or Bibliography, while APA and Harvard use References.

Are my references stored or sent anywhere?

No. All formatting happens in your browser tab. Nothing you type is transmitted, saved or logged, so unpublished drafts and unpublished sources stay private.

Conclusion

Referencing formats look arbitrary until you realise each one is just a fixed way of answering who wrote what, when, and where.
