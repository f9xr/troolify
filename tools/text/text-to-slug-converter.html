Text to Slug Converter: Build Clean URLs That Rank Better

A readable URL tells both users and search engines what a page is about before they click. This slug converter turns any headline into that clean URL string automatically - lowercase, accent-free, punctuation stripped, and ready to paste into your CMS.

What Is a URL Slug?

A slug is the tail of a URL that describes a specific page, such as best-coffee-shops-paris in example.com/guides/best-coffee-shops-paris. WordPress and other platforms generate slugs from titles, but the defaults are often messy - full of capital letters, accented characters and odd punctuation.

Great slugs are short, lowercase, use hyphens between words, and carry the page's main keyword. Words separated by hyphens are the convention Google explicitly recommends for URLs.

Useful Options

  • Lowercase only - recommended so slugs stay consistent regardless of how they are typed.
  • Use underscores - for file names or systems that expect snake_case instead of kebab-case.
  • Trim number suffix - drops patterns like -2026 from the end, handy when republishing year-marked posts.

Slug Best Practices for SEO

Keep slugs under about 60 characters, include the exact target keyword, and avoid stop words like the, and, or of when they add nothing. Do not change a slug after a post starts getting traffic unless you set up a redirect. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a URL slug?

A slug is the part of a URL that identifies a page in a readable way, such as best-coffee-shops-2026 in example.com/blog/best-coffee-shops-2026. Slugs usually use lowercase words separated by hyphens and are derived from the page title or headline.

Does a slug affect SEO?

Yes, indirectly. Google uses the URL, including the slug, when assessing and displaying search results. Short, descriptive slugs that include the target keyword tend to earn higher click-through rates and make page content easier to understand.

Why strip accents from text before making a slug?

Accented characters such as é, ö and ñ may not be encoded consistently across systems and can look ugly in URLs. Converting them to their base letters produces clean, predictable slugs that work everywhere.
