Title Case Converter: Correct Headline Capitalization Every Time

Title case is the punctuation of voice - small words stay lowercase, major words get a capital, and the first and last word always lead strong. Getting the rule right by hand is easy to botch on a busy day, and this converter applies it consistently in a split second.

About the Cases

The Title Case style capitalizes every major word while leaving a configurable list of minor words (a, an, the, of, and, for, with and friends) lowercase when they appear in the middle. The first and last words are always capitalized, which is the rule almost every style guide shares.

UPPERCASE after colon uses the same logic but also forces the word that follows a colon to start with a capital - the convention used in many book and article subtitle formats. APA-style always applies the minor-words rule and capitalizes clause starts, matching the headline style most academic and business writing expects.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Type or paste a title - your headline does not even need capitals to start with.
  2. Choose a style from the dropdown to match the guide you follow.
  3. Toggle the minor-words option if you want every word forced to a capital.
  4. Copy the result and publish it as-is.

Why Title Case Rules Hold Up

Headlines, book titles, blog post titles and file names all read better when the capitalization is predictable. Words with apostrophes (don't, it's) and hyphens (well-known, user-friendly) are handled naturally, so each meaningful part is capitalized without odd mixed-caps. Frequently Asked Questions

Which words are lowercased by the minor-words option?

Articles, conjunctions and short prepositions such as a, an, the, of, and, for, in, with, by, to and up are written in lowercase whenever they sit in the middle of a title. The first and last words are always capitalized, so titles keep their correct shape.

Why are apostrophes and hyphens treated specially?

Words like don't or user-friendly are recognized as single words, then each meaningful part is capitalized (User-Friendly, Don't). That keeps proper title case from producing awkward mixed-caps while still capitalizing each real word.
