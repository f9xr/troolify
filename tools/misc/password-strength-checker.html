Password Strength Checker: Beyond a Simple Character Count

The old advice of one uppercase, one number and one symbol misses the point: length and unpredictability do the heavy lifting. This password strength checker scores a password in real time, estimates its entropy and shows how long it would take to crack under four realistic attack scenarios.

How the score is calculated

The tool starts with the base entropy of the password - its length multiplied by the bits per character for the character sets it uses - then subtracts penalties for common passwords, repeated characters, predictable sequences, short length and low variety. The result is a practical estimate, not just a character-class checklist.

Reading the crack times

Four scenarios are shown: a throttled online attack, an unthrottled online attack, an offline attack against a slow hash, and an offline attack against a fast GPU hash. The gap between them is why server-side hashing matters so much - a password that takes years online can fall in hours if the stored hash is weak.

How to use Password Strength Checker

  1. Type or paste a password into the field.
  2. Watch the strength bar and entropy update live.
  3. Review the feedback list for specific weaknesses.
  4. Adjust the password until the score and crack time are acceptable.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Prefer a long passphrase over a short, complex jumble.
  • Never reuse a password across sites, regardless of strength.
  • Use a password manager to generate and remember strong unique values.

Key takeaways

  • Length is the biggest lever on strength.
  • Common passwords and patterns collapse otherwise high entropy.
  • Server-side hashing decides how much an offline attack costs.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to type my real password?

The check runs entirely in your browser and nothing is transmitted. Still, a genuinely sensitive password is best tested offline.

What is entropy in this context?

A measure in bits of how unpredictable the password is. Higher means more guesses are needed to find it.

Why does the crack time vary so much?

Because hashing speed varies enormously. A throttled online service is thousands of times slower than a GPU attacking a fast hash.

Does a green score mean the password is safe everywhere?

No. Strength cannot compensate for reuse or for a site that stores passwords poorly.

Is there a perfect password?

There is no single perfect value. Long, random, unique and stored in a manager is the practical ideal.
