Memorable Password Generator: Strong Passphrases You Can Recall

The strongest password is useless if you cannot remember it and dangerous if you reuse it. This memorable password generator builds passphrases from random words, giving you something that is both easy to recall and genuinely hard to crack.

Why words beat a jumble

A random string of characters is hard to memorise, so people write it down or reuse it. A sequence of unrelated words is far easier to hold in mind while still carrying high entropy: with a large wordlist each added word contributes several bits, and a handful of words already rivals a short random password.

Tuning the options

More words mean more security, so length is the main dial. The separator, capitalisation, trailing number and symbol make the phrase more compatible with site policies without meaningfully weakening it. Avoid swapping in personal words - randomness is what makes the phrase strong.

How to use Memorable Password Generator

  1. Choose how many words to use; four is a solid baseline.
  2. Pick a separator and toggle capitalisation and extras.
  3. Generate and pick a passphrase you can picture.
  4. Store it in a password manager and use it on one site only.

  • Never pick your own words; the generated randomness is the security.
  • Add words rather than relying on symbols for strength.
  • Use a different passphrase for every important account.

Key takeaways

  • Random words balance memorability and strength.
  • More words is the reliable way to increase entropy.
  • Store passphrases in a manager and never reuse them.

Frequently asked questions

How many words should I use?

Four random words is a strong baseline; five or six is better for critical accounts.

Is a passphrase stronger than a password?

A long random passphrase usually carries more entropy and is easier to remember, which makes it practically stronger for most people.

Can I choose my own words?

You can retype them, but manual choices reintroduce predictability. Prefer freshly generated phrases.

How is entropy estimated?

By multiplying the number of words by the bits per word in the list, plus any added number or symbol.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Passphrases are generated entirely in your browser.
