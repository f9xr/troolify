The area is where velocity hides

Every airflow statement in a duct is CFM divided by area. Knowing the true cross-section of a run - round or rectangular - unlocks velocity checks and sizing math that otherwise rely on a guessed number.

Round area

Round duct area is πd²/4. In inches, a 12-inch duct is 113.1 in², or 0.785 sq ft - enough for about 785 CFM at 1000 fpm.

Rectangular area

Rectangular area is simply width times height. The same 113.1 in² can appear as a 14 by 8, a 16 by 7, or any other pair - which is why rectangular sizing pairs with the Rectangular Duct Size and Equivalent Diameter tools.

Turning area into decisions

Feed the square-foot value into the Duct Velocity calculator to see what the run actually does at design CFM, or invert it with the placement in the Duct Size Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does duct area affect airflow?

Directly - airflow at a given velocity is area times velocity. Double the area and the same velocity carries twice the CFM.

How do I find the area of a round duct?

Square the inside diameter in inches, multiply by π (3.1416), and divide by 4 to get square inches; divide by 144 for square feet.

Should I use inside or outside dimensions?

Use inside (free) dimensions - airflow sees the internal bore, not the sheet-listed nominal size.

Why does my duct area feel too small?

Rectangular ducts are specified by nominal dimensions and often line with restrictions like dampers or splitters. Measure the free opening, not the label.

Conclusion

Cross-sectional area is the hinge between geometry and performance. Compute it, label the units, and every velocity check downstream is trustworthy.
