The metric-to-imperial direction

Dividing m³/h by 60 gives m³/min, then scaling to cubic feet yields the CFM figure. The consolidated factor is 0.5885778 - exactly the inverse of 1.6990108 used for the reverse direction.

Keeping one basis per stream

European AHUs quote m³/h; North American duct calculators expect CFM. Convert the fan rating to match the duct calculator's basis once, label it, and never re-convert in the other direction mid-design.

The L/s cross-check

One m³/h equals 0.2778 L/s. Reporting L/s alongside CFM lets you sanity-check against grille catalogues that use metric ratings.

Round-tripping

Convert a number here, then back with the CFM to m3/h converter - the factor inverses cleanly and returns the original value, proving the arithmetic.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many CFM is 2500 m3/h?

1,471.44 CFM (2500 × 0.5885778).

Is m3/h the same as m3/min?

No - divide m³/h by 60 to get m³/min. Mislabelling the time base is a very common airflow error.

Should I use CFM or m3/h?

Use whichever the rest of the system and its codes use. Mixing units within one design is where errors appear.

Does density change the conversion?

No - this is volume-flow conversion. If you need standard-air mass flow, apply density corrections separately at the stated temperature and altitude.

Conclusion

Metric airflow converts to CFM cleanly - once. Pick a basis, convert, label, and keep the whole branch consistent.
