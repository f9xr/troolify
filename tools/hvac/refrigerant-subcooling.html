What subcooling verifies

Subcooling measures how far below condensation temperature the liquid refrigerant is when it leaves the condenser. Enough subcooling guarantees a solid liquid column to the metering device; too little warns of flash gas and starving evaporators.

Measuring it

Read the liquid line temperature at the condenser outlet and subtract it from the saturation temperature corresponding to your high-side pressure. TXV systems are typically charged to a specified subcooling, making this the go-to number on the liquid side.

Reading the sign

Negative subcooling means the thermometer and pressure gauge disagree or the liquid is picking up heat. Low subcooling commonly flags undercharge; chronic high subcooling often points to overcharge or a fouled condenser coil shedding heat too fast.

The charge triangle

Subcooling and Refrigerant Superheat are the two handles of charge diagnosis; the Refrigeration Capacity estimate turns the findings into capacity consequences.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should subcooling be?

Typical targets are 8-15 F on TXV systems - but the manufacturers spec for the model and ambient is what should run the charging decision.

What does low subcooling mean?

Usually an undercharge or a TXV driven condenser - the liquid is leaving too close to saturation and risks flashing before expansion.

Why measure at the condenser outlet?

That is where the liquid leaves the condenser. Measuring further down the line adds line heat gain and shrinks the true subcooling by the gain.

Can subcooling be too high?

Yes - chronically high subcooling often signals overcharge or a blocked/oversized condenser that condenses far more than needed.

Conclusion

Subcooling tests the liquid side the way superheat tests the vapour side. Measure both, read the manufacturer spec, and only then discuss the charge.
