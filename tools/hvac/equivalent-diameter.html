Moving between shapes

Rectangular ducts fit building voids; round ducts are cheaper per pound of material and quieter. Converting a rectangular size to its round equal-friction equivalent lets a designer jump between systems while keeping similar pressure-drop behavior at the same airflow.

The ASHRAE formula

The equal-friction diameter is 1.30 × (ab)^0.625 / (a+b)^0.25 with a and b in inches. It yields the round diameter that produces the same friction loss per unit length at the same CFM - a sizing bridge, not a physical truth about surface area.

What equal-friction means

Equal-friction says the two shapes behave similarly along a clean straight run; it does not equalize transitions, branch loses, or how each shape handles elbows. Treat the bridge number as a starting point the friction tool should confirm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is equal-friction equivalent diameter?

The round duct diameter that gives the same friction loss per 100 ft as the rectangular duct at the same airflow, via 1.30(ab)^0.625/(a+b)^0.25.

Is a 16x8 duct equal to an 11-inch round?

Roughly - 16×8 computes to about 11.0 inches equivalent; note the actual area (128 in² = 0.89 sq ft) is larger than an 11-inch circle (95 in²), which is why equal-friction, not equal-area, is the right comparison.

Why use equal-friction instead of equal area?

Friction, not heat loss, dominates duct performance; equal-friction keeps pressure behavior matched so fan selection remains valid across shape changes.

Does this work for flat-oval duct?

Flat-oval has its own equivalent formulas; apply them with the same caveat - transitions and elbows still need a full friction check.

Conclusion

Equal-friction equivalence lets rectangular and round duct designs share a common language. Use the bridge number, then confirm with real friction math.
