Thermal Unit Conversion

Ready - convert units in this tab.

Unit conversion is exact by definition; the underlying capacity value still comes from equipment data or an earlier calculation, so verify the source before sizing. Full disclaimer.

Why unit conversion needs discipline

HVAC capacity appears in BTU/h, tons, watts, kilowatts, MBH and even horsepower depending on the vendor and the country. Misreading MBH as BTU/h - or kilowatts as BTU/h - is one of the most common sizing errors. This calculator keeps every step transparent.

Anchoring to watts

The converter expresses every unit in watts first (1 BTU/h = 0.29307107 W, 1 ton = 3,516.85 W), then re-expresses the result in your target unit. Because the anchor is always the same, round-trip conversions (BTU/h to kW then back) return the same figure.

Where each unit appears

Tons and BTU/h dominate North American split-system ratings; kW dominates European and inverter catalogues; MBH is common in boilers and rooftop units. Use the BTU to Tons and Tons to BTU tools for the two conversions you will make most often.

Verifying a stated capacity

Always convert the value on the nameplate rather than a rounded brochure number. A 36,000 BTU/h condenser is 3 tons; a 3 kW heat pump is roughly 10,240 BTU/h - converted correctly before you compare against a design load.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is MBH the same as kBtu/h?

Yes - both represent 1,000 BTU/h. HVAC convention shortens this to MBH, and the 'M' refers to a thousand in Roman numeral style.

How do I convert BTU/h to kW?

Divide by 3,412.14, or multiply by 0.00029307107. The calculator does this for you across all nine units.

Is 1 horsepower a useful HVAC unit?

Rarely for capacity - hp is mainly a compressor motor rating (1 hp = 0.746 kW). Capacity tonnage is the correct thermal rating.

Can conversions change my design basis?

No. Conversion is dimensional only - the underlying capacity is unchanged. The risk is misreading the source rating, not the arithmetic.

Conclusion

Keep one reliable converter and a copy of the nameplate. Thermal capacity bought and sold in mixed units must be compared on a single basis.
