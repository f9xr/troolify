Start with geometry

Duct sizing begins with the continuity equation: area equals airflow divided by velocity. Pick the CFM you must deliver and the face velocity you can tolerate (noisy rooms need low velocity), and the required round diameter follows directly.

Choosing a velocity

Low velocities (300-700 fpm) suit residential and quiet spaces; higher velocities (up to 1,000+ fpm) fit concealed utility areas and high-pressure systems. Each doubling of velocity roughly quadruples noise energy transmitted to occupied rooms.

Standard sizes

Duct comes in fixed diameters (4 to 48 inches in 2-inch steps), so the theoretical value is rounded up to the nearest standard size. Rounding up changes the actual face velocity - the tool re-checks it for you.

The second half

Geometry gives you size; friction gives you the pressure cost. Run the result through the Duct Friction Loss calculator so the selected diameter is reconciled with the fan's static capability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size duct do I need for 600 CFM?

At a typical 800 fpm you need 0.75 sq ft, a theoretical 11.7-inch diameter - so a 12-inch round duct at roughly 765 fpm.

Why use velocity to size ducts?

Velocity couples airflow with noise and erosion. Design velocity caps are what make a system quiet and stable while the area equation handles the capacity.

Rectangular or round?

Round ducts carry more flow per square inch of material and are quieter. Rectangular fits tight wall and ceiling cavities. Convert between them with the Equivalent Diameter tool.

Does duct size affect airflow?

Yes - as a duct system's resistance grows the fan delivers less at the same static pressure. Sizing must keep friction within the fan's capability.

Conclusion

Pick the CFM, pick the velocity, and the round diameter leaves almost no room for guesswork. Friction is the next fight - check it before committing to a run.
