One state, many properties

Psychrometrics describes moist air: given one dry-bulb and its relative humidity, the dew point, humidity ratio, enthalpy and wet-bulb are all fixed. That is the power of the chart - and of this calculator, which computes the same properties algebraically.

Dew point and humidity ratio

Dew point is where condensation begins on a surface - critical for coil and window condensation risk. Humidity ratio (kg of water per kg of dry air) is the mass-language version of humidity that latent-load math actually uses.

Enthalpy - the air's energy budget

Moist-air enthalpy is the sensible heat of the air plus the latent heat of its moisture. It appears directly in coil load equations (BTU/h = 4.5 × CFM × Δh) and in the Fume Hood Exhaust Load tool.

Why wet bulb matters

Wet-bulb is the thermodynamic enthalpy anchor - evaporation cooling and cooling-tower performance depend on it. The Stull formula approximates it from dry-bulb and RH for HVAC ranges without a psychrometer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the dew point formula?

The Magnus formula: with gamma = ln(RH/100) + 17.62T/(243.12+T), dew point = 243.12 gamma/(17.62 - gamma), using Celsius.

How do I find enthalpy of moist air?

h = 1.006T + w(2501 + 1.86T) kJ/kg, where w is the humidity ratio in kg/kg and T is Celsius. Multiply by 0.43021 for Btu/lb.

What does humidity ratio mean?

It is the mass of water vapour per mass of dry air (kg/kg or lb/lb) - the property latent-load equations use, unlike relative humidity which is pressure-dependent.

Is the wet bulb estimate accurate?

Within typical HVAC ranges the Stull approximation is within about a degree; for design certification, verify on a full psychrometric chart at your altitude.

Conclusion

Every moist-air property hangs off two measurements. Document the state, compute the rest, and the latent half of your load stops being a guess.
