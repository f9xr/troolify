What this load calculator does

Before a full Manual J or heat-gain survey, engineers and contractors need a transparent, component-based sketch of a space's cooling and heating demand. This tool sums glass, people, equipment, lighting, infiltration and an envelope allowance into a single preliminary load - with the sensible/latent split preserved so latent-heavy spaces (kitchens, gyms, labs) are not undersized.

The preliminary factors

Each component uses a documented factor: glazing at 30 BTU/h per square foot, occupants at 250 sensible plus 150 latent BTU/h, electrical load at 3.413 BTU/h per watt, and infiltration at 1.08 BTU/h per CFM per degree Fahrenheit. The envelope allowance of 12 BTU/h per square foot stands in for wall and roof conduction until a proper survey refines it.

Why tons matter

Equipment is sold by nominal ton (12,000 BTU/h), so translating the preliminary load into tons tells you which standard unit bracket to start from. Thermal kilowatts put the same number on power and electricity bills. Neither replaces the manufacturer curve - it simply brackets the search.

Where the estimate stops

Orientation, sun shading, roof solar gain, duct losses, blower heat and humidity from ventilation air all fall outside this sketch. Treat the result as a sanity check, then commission a full load calculation before committing to equipment.

What to do next

Pair the load with the Heat Pump Operating Cost calculator once a capacity and COP are chosen, or normalise it with the BTU per Square Foot tool to compare against similar spaces.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this a Manual J load calculation?

No. It is a preliminary component aggregate used to sanity-check sizing. Manual J accounts for orientation, construction type, duct losses and infiltration rates in full detail.

What does 1 ton of cooling mean?

One refrigeration ton equals 12,000 BTU/h - the cooling effect of melting one ton of ice in 24 hours. It is the standard rating unit for residential and light commercial HVAC.

Why split sensible and latent?

Latent load is moisture (condensation work) and needs a coil designed for it. A gym full of people needs far more latent capacity than an identical empty room of the same floor area.

Is 12 BTU/h per square foot enough for the shell?

It is a reasonable envelope allowance for preliminary sketching. Actual conduction depends on climate, insulation, window-to-wall ratio and orientation - refine it with a proper survey.

Conclusion

Component aggregation converts guesses into a defensible first number. Use it to bracket equipment size, then hand the build over to a full load calculation.
