The refrigerant-side equation

Refrigerant-side capacity is mass flow times enthalpy difference: Q = ̋ × (h₊ - h₋). Measure or infer each state point, multiply, and the cooling effect emerges in BTU/h without any air-side guessing.

Where the enthalpy comes from

Suction and discharge states are read from pressure-temperature-enthalpy tables for the actual refrigerant (R-22, R-410A, R-134a, R-32...). Line temperatures and gauge pressures identify which enthalpy to use - measured values, not assumed defaults.

Fluid-side honesty

This is an evaporator/condenser performance estimate, not a charge target. A measured capacity far from trust means the log deserves a full diagnostic review - superheat, subcooling, pressures and airflow together.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is refrigeration capacity measured?

Capacity = refrigerant mass flow (lb/min) × enthalpy difference (Btu/lb) × 60, or via the air side as CFM × 4.5 × enthalpy change of the air.

Why enthalpy and not temperature?

Enthalpy carries the latent component of the refrigerant's change of state, which temperature alone misses when the refrigerant is condensing or evaporating.

Can I use this to charge a system?

No. Capacity estimates support diagnosis; charging follows manufacturer subcooling/superheat targets applied by a licensed technician.

Which enthalpy units does the calculator want?

Btu per pound. If your table lists kJ/kg, divide by 2.326 to convert before entering.

Conclusion

Refrigerant-side capacity is the cleanest statement of what a cycle can do. Get the state points right and the arithmetic is exact; keep away from charge-setting with an estimate.
