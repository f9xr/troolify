Open-Drive Belt Pitch Length

Ready - belt length estimate in this tab.

Pitch length from open-drive geometry. Stock belts come in fixed nominal lengths - pick the closest and verify the resulting centre-distance adjustment. Full disclaimer.

The open-drive length

For two sheaves on parallel shafts with no idler, the belt pitch length is 2C + 1.57(D + d) + (D - d)²/4C - straight runs plus the two arcs around the sheaves. Measure centre distance and pitch diameters, and the belt length is arithmetic.

Why wrap angle matters

Wrap angle is how much of each sheave the belt actually contacts, and it sets grip. Small sheave wrap below about 120° starves the drive of friction - signalled by squeal and shortened belt life. An idler or a bigger centre distance fixes it.

From pitch length to stock

Belts are sold by nominal outside length family (A/4L, B/5L), while this calculation is pitch length. Pick the standard belt at or above the pitch length and allow for slight centre-distance adjustment to take up the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate V-belt length?

For two equal-height pulleys: L = 2C + 1.57(D + d) + (D - d)²/(4C), with D, d the pitch diameters and C the centre distance.

What is a good wrap angle?

Aim for at least 120 degrees on the small sheave. Below that, belt slip and wear climb quickly - consider an idler or tensioner.

Pitch length vs outside length?

Pitch length is the belt's neutral-axis length; outside length is what the part number family is based on. They differ by about the belt section height.

Can I adjust centre distance to fit a stock belt?

Yes - within the drive's take-up range. Belt drives are usually designed with a little centre-distance adjustment room to land on a stock belt.

Conclusion

Belt length and wrap angles come straight from geometry. Compute, compare to stock, and verify the small-sheave wrap before ordering.
