Normalised Airflow

Ready - airflow density check in this tab.

CFM per square foot is a normalization metric. Codes and standards (ASHRAE 62.1, IMC) define ventilation by occupancy and floor area - use those, not this density, for compliance. Full disclaimer.

Why normalize airflow

Raw CFM is hard to compare across rooms of different sizes. Dividing airflow by served floor area produces CFM per square foot - a density you can compare against published ranges and other spaces in the same building.

What typical values look like

Commercial ventilation commonly sits between 0.5 and 1.5 CFM/sf. Below that, the space is probably relying on natural infiltration; far above it, you are in lab-exhaust or process territory. Each is fine in context.

Codes, not densities

ASHRAE 62.1 and the IMC base ventilation on occupancy counts and floor area, not on a single CFM/sf rule. This tool is for noticing anomalies - a 50,000 CFM AHU over a 1,000 sq ft room warrants a question; it is not automatically wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the normal CFM per square foot?

Roughly 0.5-1.5 CFM/sf for occupied commercial space. Homes vary widely depending on whether it is supply, return, or mechanical ventilation-only.

Is more CFM per square foot always better?

No - excessive airflow drafts, noise, and undermines humidity control. Match the rate to the occupancy and code requirement.

How many people can my airflow support?

Refer to ASHRAE 62.1, which gives outdoor air per person plus per area. Density alone can't answer the occupancy question.

Can I use CFM/sf to size a fan?

Only as a first approximation. Real sizing uses design airflow from load/ACH calculations and the fan curve against the duct system static pressure.

Conclusion

CFM per square foot turns an abstract airflow figure into a comparable density - a quick diagnostic, never the final code argument.
