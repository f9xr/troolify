Opening-Average Velocity

Ready - opening-average velocity in this tab.

This is a documented average - the actual distribution across the sash opening varies and should be verified with a traverse. It sets no pass/fail criteria. Full disclaimer.

One number, produced honestly

Average face velocity is the exhaust airflow divided by the clear sash area: the hood moves 750 CFM through a 2.5 sq ft opening at 300 fpm (well above the usual operating window) or through a taut slot where flow reads differently. Compute the average first, then look at the distribution.

Design context, not pass/fail

Operating guidance commonly lands between 80 and 120 fpm, but local codes and the hood type set the real requirement. This tool reports the number against that context and deliberately refuses to invent a verdict - the traverse and the governing standard decide.

Sash area drives everything

Because velocity is airflow divided by area, lowering the sash raises the average velocity at the same CFM. That relationship underpins energy-saving variable-volume exhaust systems - and it is why the sash measurement must be the clear opening, not the frame.

Frequently Asked Questions

What face velocity should a fume hood have?

Common operating guidance is 80-120 fpm for conventional hoods at the design sash height; the governing standard and hood type determine the enforceable value.

How is face velocity measured?

As an average - airflow over clear sash area - ideally confirmed by traversing the opening in grid cells and averaging the readings.

Does raising the sash change velocity?

Yes - a taller sash means more area, so average velocity drops at a fixed CFM. Variable-volume exhaust raises fan speed to compensate.

Is 100 FPM always fine?

Not automatically - containment also depends on the traverse distribution, hood geometry, room air currents and the process. Average velocity is the check's first line, not its conclusion.

Conclusion

Face velocity starts as division and ends as a documented average. Pair it with a traverse and the standard before declaring any hood good.
