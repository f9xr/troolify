Crypto ROI Calculator: Measure Trades the Way Investors Do

"I made money" is not a number. Return on investment is - and comparing trades, coins, and entry styles is impossible without it. This crypto ROI calculator turns your investment amount, buy price and sell price into a clean ROI percentage, the profit in dollars, and the break-even price you need to actually get your money back.

How to Use the Crypto ROI Calculator

  1. Enter the amount you invested in dollars.
  2. Enter the buy price per coin so the tool can work out how many units you hold.
  3. Enter the sell or target price - use a target to plan, or the current price to check an open position.
  4. Optionally set a fee percentage to see the effect of exchange costs.

The result shows your units bought, sale value, ROI percentage, net profit and break-even price side by side.

The ROI Formula

ROI = (sale value − invested − fees) / invested × 100. Sale value is units bought (invested ÷ buy price) times the sell price. The break-even price is the sell price where this equals zero, given the fee on both sides - with no fees, it is simply your buy price.

Using ROI for Targets

A ROI of 100% on 4 coins worth the same total is not a fair comparison - comparing percentages gives you a level playing field. Run the tool with a target price first; if the ROI meets your plan, fine. If not, you know before you risk a cent whether the trade actually clears your bar after fees.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is ROI calculated for crypto?

ROI = (sale value - investment - fees) / investment × 100. The sale value is the units bought (investment ÷ buy price) multiplied by the sell price, and fees are subtracted from both sides of the trade.

What is a break-even price?

The break-even price is the sell price at which your net return is exactly zero after fees. Selling above it means a profit; selling below it means a loss. With zero fees it equals your buy price.

Should I include fees in my ROI?

Yes, if you want an honest picture. Excluding fees flatters the result - a position that looks mildly in profit can be a small loss once fees on both the buy and sell side are deducted.
