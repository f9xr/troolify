Video File Size Calculator: Know the Size Before You Render

Nothing eats storage and bandwidth quite like video. A single hour of 4K footage can land around 15 GB, while a modest 720p edit fits on a USB stick. This calculator estimates file size from bitrate and duration - or works backwards to the maximum bitrate for a fixed size limit - while accounting for audio and container overhead.

How the Numbers Are Calculated

File size is bitrate multiplied by time: (total bitrate in Mbps × 1,000,000 ÷ 8) × seconds. With the new Audio bitrate field, you can add or remove the soundtrack - video-only bitrate targets are still shown, but real exports are usually video plus audio. The Quality target presets give a quick starting point, while the inverse mode works backwards: enter a size limit (GB) and the calculator reveals the maximum total bitrate you can afford for that duration.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Choose the direction - "File size from bitrate + duration" or "Max bitrate from a size limit".
  2. Set your duration in minutes.
  3. Forward mode: enter your video bitrate (or pick a resolution preset) and choose an audio bitrate (128 kbps is standard for AAC). The result shows the estimated total size, total bitrate, and MB/s.
  4. Inverse mode: enter your size limit in GB and the calculator gives the maximum total bitrate, the video bitrate left after audio, and whether audio already exceeds the limit.
  5. Scan the chart - it shows the size for common resolutions using your total bitrate, so you can compare targets at a glance.

Picking the Right Bitrate

For H.264, 1080p sits comfortably around 8 Mbps, 720p around 5 Mbps and 4K around 35 Mbps. Double those for high-motion content such as sports or action footage. Newer codecs like HEVC (H.265) and AV1 can deliver similar quality at roughly half the bitrate. Remember that audio adds 0.1–0.3 Mbps on top of that, so do not forget to include it if you want a realistic number.

Planning Storage and Delivery

Budget drives, estimate upload times and choose delivery resolutions with confidence. A 1-hour lecture at 720p (~2.25 GB video-only) becomes closer to 2.3 GB once audio is included. If you are exporting vertical formats for Shorts, Reels or TikTok, first size your dimensions with the Video Aspect Ratio Calculator, and if you are about to record a demo screen capture, grab it with the Screen Recorder.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the file size of a video?

Multiply the total bitrate in bits per second by the duration in seconds, then divide by 8 to get bytes. The Troolify calculator adds video and audio together automatically.

What bitrate should I use for 1080p video?

About 8 Mbps is standard for 1080p H.264. Add around 128 kbps for AAC audio, so total ~8.13 Mbps - that is close to what the calculator gives for 10 minutes (~609 MB).

Why is my 4K video file so large?

4K needs roughly 35 Mbps of video bitrate to keep detail. With audio that is ~35.13 Mbps, which equates to around 15.8 GB per hour. Using HEVC or AV1 often cuts this by 30–50% at similar visual quality.
