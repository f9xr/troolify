Video File Size Calculator: Know the Size Before You Render

Nothing eats storage and bandwidth quite like video. A single hour of 4K footage can land around 15 GB, while a modest 720p edit of the same length fits on a USB stick. This calculator tells you exactly what any video will weigh before you spend hours waiting on the render.

The Simple Formula Behind the Number

File size comes down to two inputs: bitrate (how many bits per second the footage needs) and duration. Convert the bitrate to bytes per second, then multiply by the duration in seconds: file size = (bitrate in Mbps × 1,000,000) ÷ 8 × seconds. A 10-minute clip at 8 Mbps works out to about 600 MB.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Enter your bitrate - or pick a resolution preset and the suggested bitrate is applied.
  2. Set the duration in minutes.
  3. Read the result - file size, plus a per-hour reference.
  4. Scan the chart to compare 4K, 1440p, 1080p and lower at the same length.

Picking the Right Bitrate

For H.264, 1080p sits comfortably around 8 Mbps, 720p around 5 Mbps and 4K around 35 Mbps - double those for high-motion content like sports or action footage. Newer codecs such as HEVC (H.265) and AV1 deliver the same quality at roughly half the bitrate, so files can be much smaller for the same resolution.

Planning Storage and Delivery

Producers use this math to budget hard drives, estimate upload times and choose delivery resolutions. A 1-hour lecture at 720p (~2.25 GB) is far friendlier for a learning platform than the same lecture at 4K (~15.75 GB). Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the file size of a video?

Multiply the bitrate in bits per second by the duration in seconds, then divide by 8 to get bytes. Divide again by 1,000,000 for megabytes. A 10-minute video at 8 Mbps is about 600 MB.

What bitrate should I use for 1080p video?

Around 8 Mbps is a solid all-round target for 1080p on modern codecs like H.264. Go higher (up to about 12 Mbps) for action-packed or highly detailed footage, and lower if storage matters more than peak quality.

Why is my 4K video file so large?

4K has four times the pixels of 1080p, so it typically needs a bitrate around 35 Mbps to keep detail. At 35 Mbps, every hour of footage is about 15.75 GB - that is why 4K producers rely on HEVC or AV1 to cut size without cutting quality.
