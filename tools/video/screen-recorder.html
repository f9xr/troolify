Browser-Based Screen Recording, No Install Required

The Screen Recorder captures your whole screen, a single window or one browser tab using the capture engine already built into your browser - then packages the footage into a downloadable WebM file when you hit stop. There is no installer, no account and no upload: the stream stays in the tab until you download it.

How to Record Your Screen in Four Steps

  1. Click Start recording and pick what to share - entire screen, a specific window, or a single tab. Your browser shows its own permission prompt, and the tab you are recording stays fully interactive.
  2. Adjust the options first if you care about size - frame rate and quality target are set before you start (details below).
  3. Do your thing - the status line counts up so you know how much footage you are capturing.
  4. Click Stop & download, watch the playback preview, then save the .webm file with the Download button. Clear discards the recording and resets the tool.

Frame Rate, Quality and System Audio

Three options sit above the record button, and they make the difference between a 40 MB and a 3 MB file for the same ten minutes of footage.

  • Frame rate - 30 fps for smooth motion, 15 fps for talking heads and demos, 5 fps for slide decks where only the changes matter. Frame rate is also passed to the capture request as an ideal, so the browser can downscale if it needs to.
  • Quality target - an approximate video bitrate. Leave it on browser default for general use, drop to ~2.5 Mbps for a walkthrough, or push ~10 Mbps when the recording is mostly crisp text and code.
  • Record system audio - on by default. Availability depends on the browser and the source: Chrome and Edge can share a tab's own audio and, on some platforms, full system audio; Firefox usually captures tab audio only. If audio sharing is blocked, switch the toggle off and the video still records cleanly.

The recorder negotiates the best WebM codec your browser supports, falling back from VP9 to VP8 and then to a plain video/webm stream, so the file plays back in Chrome, Edge and Firefox without a transcode.

Why Browser Capture Beats Installing OBS

A full broadcast-style setup is overkill when you just need a corner of the screen for a bug report or a short walkthrough. In-browser capture is instant, needs no plugins, cannot leak a stream key, and keeps the media local until you choose to download it. That makes it the right tool for support tickets, quick product walkthroughs, and async updates to a teammate who is not free right now.

Troubleshooting Blank Tabs and Missing Audio

Two problems come up more than any other, and both have the same cause - the source you picked.

  • The captured tab goes black or flickers. Some browsers and GPU drivers cannot composite a protected or hardware-accelerated tab into the capture stream. Capture the window instead of the tab, or capture the whole screen and crop later.
  • No sound in the recording. Check the audio toggle first, then remember that mutes, DRM-protected players and some conferencing apps suppress capture at the source. Recording a single window rather than the full screen is the most reliable way to keep audio.
  • The Start button does nothing. The tool tells you why on the status line: an insecure (http://) connection cannot capture at all, and browsers without getDisplayMedia - older Safari, most in-app webviews - cannot either.

If a capture stalls after a long session, stop and restart the recording rather than leaving it running: chunks are flushed every second, but a stuck encoder cannot recover on its own.

What to Do With the File Next

The WebM you download is a finished video file, but it is a delivery format rather than an editing one. Before sending it to a client or uploading it somewhere that only accepts MP4, remux or convert it. If you also need to shrink it, work out the target bitrate for your duration with the Video File Size Calculator, then check the output dimensions against your platform with the Video Aspect Ratio Calculator so it does not get letterboxed. For a still frame pulled straight out of the footage, use the Image Compressor to get the screenshot under an upload limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What format is the recording saved as?

WebM (Matroska video with a VP8 or VP9 codec), the standard output of the browser's MediaRecorder. The recorder picks the highest codec your browser supports and always produces a WebM file with the .webm extension.

Is my recording uploaded anywhere?

No. Capture happens through the browser's own screen-sharing API, the chunks are assembled into a blob in the page, and the file is only written to disk when you click Download. Nothing is sent to a server, and there is no account to create.

Why is there no sound in my recording?

System audio capture depends on the browser and the source. Chrome and Edge can share tab audio and, on some platforms, full system audio; Firefox generally captures tab audio only. Muted tabs, DRM-protected players and some conferencing apps also block capture at the source. Recording a single window rather than the whole screen is the most reliable option, and you can confirm the audio toggle is on before you start.

The tab I am recording turns black - what should I record instead?

Capture the window containing the tab, or the whole screen, rather than the tab itself. Some browsers and GPU drivers cannot composite a hardware-accelerated or protected tab into the capture stream, which produces a black or flickering recording. A window capture normally works where a tab capture fails.

How do I make the recording file smaller?

Lower the frame rate and the quality target before you start - 15 fps at ~2.5 Mbps is a good balance for a talking-head walkthrough, while 5 fps is enough for slide decks. Longer recordings also need remuxing or conversion to MP4 if the destination only accepts that format.

Does the Troolify screen recorder work on mobile?

The tool relies on getDisplayMedia, which Android's Chrome supports from recent versions but which iOS Safari does not implement at all. On desktop Chrome, Edge and Firefox it works with no install. If capture is unavailable, the status line tells you so instead of failing silently.
