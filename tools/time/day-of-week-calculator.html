Day of Week Calculator: Which Day Was That Date

Whether you are checking the weekday of a historical date, planning a date years ahead or confirming the day a contract was signed, this day of week calculator gives the answer instantly, along with the weekday number, ISO week and day of the year.

A question calendars answer slowly

Weekdays repeat on a seven-day cycle, which makes long-range questions surprisingly error-prone to do mentally, especially across leap years. The tool reads the calendar date you enter and reports the day name computed from the proleptic Gregorian calendar, the same system used for standard date arithmetic. The weekday number counts from Monday as one through to Sunday as seven, matching the ISO convention.

When you need the weekday

People use it to check whether a future deadline lands on a weekend, to settle trivia about what day a famous event happened, to find out the weekday of a birthday or anniversary, and to plan events on a favoured day. Historians and genealogists also use it to confirm dates in old documents.

How to use Day of Week Calculator

  1. Enter a date - it can be in the past or the future.
  2. Press Find the day.
  3. Read the weekday name and the supporting numbers.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Weekday number 1 is Monday and 7 is Sunday, following ISO practice.
  • Cross-check the week number if you are planning against a schedule.
  • Remember leap years shift weekdays by two days instead of one.

Frequently asked questions

Can I check a future date?

Yes - the calculator works for any valid Gregorian date, past or future.

What does the weekday number mean?

It follows ISO numbering, where Monday is 1 and Sunday is 7.

Does it account for leap years?

Yes. The underlying calendar arithmetic handles leap years correctly.

Which calendar system is used?

The proleptic Gregorian calendar, the international standard for civil dates.

Do you store my date?

No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser.

Conclusion
