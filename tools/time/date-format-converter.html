Date Format Converter: One Date, Every Common Format

The same date looks completely different depending on who is reading it. This date format converter takes one date and returns it in ISO 8601, RFC 2822, US, European, long written and Unix forms at the same time, all ready to copy.

Why the same day looks so different

ISO 8601 puts the year first, which sorts cleanly and avoids any ambiguity. RFC 2822 is the format used in email headers and is always expressed in UTC. American short dates lead with the month while most of Europe and the UK lead with the day, the single most common source of confusion in spreadsheets. Unix timestamps count the seconds since 1 January 1970, and are what computers store internally.

Picking the right one

Use ISO 8601 in databases, filenames and log lines because it sorts correctly. Use RFC 2822 when you are building or debugging email headers. Use the US or European form when writing to a human audience in that region. Unix timestamps are the right choice when calling an API or storing a moment in code, and the long form is best for prose.

How to use Date Format Converter

Frequently asked questions

Which format should I use for a database?

ISO 8601 sorts correctly as plain text, which makes it ideal for databases, logs and filenames.

What is RFC 2822?

A date format used in email headers and some web standards, always shown in UTC here.

Is the Unix timestamp in seconds or milliseconds?

Both are shown - seconds for most APIs, milliseconds for JavaScript and some systems.

Can I add a time?

Yes. Enter a time and it is used in the datetime, RFC and Unix outputs.

Is anything uploaded?

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser.
