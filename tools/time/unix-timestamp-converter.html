Unix Timestamp Converter: Make Sense of Every Epoch Second

Every developer eventually meets an opaque number like 1785307500 in a database, log file, or API response. It is a Unix timestamp - the seconds elapsed since 1 January 1970 UTC - and this tool translates it to a readable local and UTC date, or converts any date back the other way.

What Is a Unix Timestamp?

Also called epoch time, the Unix timestamp counts seconds starting at 00:00:00 UTC on 1 January 1970 (the "epoch"). Because the count is identical everywhere on Earth, timestamps are timezone-independent and unambiguous - the same integer means the same instant in Tokyo, London, or New York.

Some systems store milliseconds instead of seconds (the value multiplied by 1,000). The tool always assumes seconds; if a value seems exactly 1,000× too large, divide by 1,000 before converting.

How to Use the Converter

  1. Timestamp to date - type or paste epoch seconds into the first field; local and UTC dates appear instantly.
  2. Date to timestamp - pick any date and time in the second field; the matching epoch seconds fill the first field live.
  3. Now - hit the "Use current timestamp" button to jump to the present in both formats.

A always-live card keeps the current epoch second ticking below the results, ideal for testing API calls that expect recent timestamps.

UTC vs Local: Why Both Views Matter

The same timestamp prints differently depending on your timezone offset. The converter shows UTC (the fixed reference) and local time (what a calendar on your wall says) side by side, so you can confirm which one your database row or log line is describing before you trust it in code.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Unix timestamp?

A Unix timestamp (also called epoch time) is a large integer that counts the number of seconds that have passed since 1 January 1970 00:00:00 UTC. Unix timestamps are timezone-independent, which makes them ideal for storing time in databases and APIs.

How do I convert a Unix timestamp to a normal date?

Type or paste the timestamp in seconds into the first field and the converted local and UTC dates appear instantly. The reverse direction works too: pick a date and the tool returns the exact epoch seconds representing that instant.

Why do my timestamps display differently in UTC vs local time?

A timestamp represents a single instant in time. UTC is the fixed global reference, while your local time is that same instant shifted by your timezone offset (plus daylight saving where applicable). Both views are shown together so you can always cross-check.
