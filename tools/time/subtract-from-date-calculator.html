Subtract from a Date Calculator: Jump Backward on the Calendar Instantly

Need to figure out what date it was thirty days ago, or when a subscription started a year before today? This calculator takes any start date and subtracts days, weeks, months or years from it, giving you the exact resulting date and its weekday in milliseconds.

How It Works

Select a starting date, type the amount to subtract, and pick the unit from the dropdown. The result appears instantly below, showing both the ISO date and a human-friendly long format. You can enter any whole number, including negatives, which effectively adds time instead.

Month and Year Rollover

The calculator correctly handles months that have different numbers of days. Subtracting one month from March 31 lands on February 28 (or 29 in a leap year). Subtracting one year from February 29 in a leap year gives February 28, since the target year is not a leap year. These edge cases are handled automatically and match what you would find in standard calendar applications.

Practical Use Cases

Subtracting dates is useful when you need to find past deadlines, verify when an event happened, calculate backdated contract start dates, or simply satisfy curiosity about a specific date in the past. It also pairs naturally with the Add to a Date Calculator for full two-way date arithmetic.

How to Use the Calculator

  1. Pick a start date by clicking the date field or typing directly.
  2. Enter an amount - positive values subtract time, negative values add time.
  3. Choose the unit from the dropdown: days, weeks, months or years.
  4. Read the result below, showing the resulting date, weekday and formatted versions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I subtract a month from March 31?

Subtracting one month from March 31 gives you February 28 (or February 29 in a leap year). The calculator clamps the day to the last valid day of the target month so you never land on an impossible date like February 30 or February 31.

What does subtracting by months do to the day number?

When the target month has fewer days than the original day number, the result is clamped to the last day of that month. For example, subtracting one month from March 30 gives February 28 or 29 depending on the leap year. The day is always adjusted to be a valid calendar date.

Can I subtract backwards to find a past date?

Yes. Enter any positive amount and the calculator subtracts it from the start date, giving you a date in the past. This is useful for finding deadlines, renewal dates or historical references. You can also enter a negative number to add time instead.

Is anything stored online?

No. Every calculation is performed entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing is transmitted to any server, nothing is stored in cookies or local storage, and nothing is logged. Your privacy is fully preserved.
