What Is a YouTube Timestamp Link, and Why Does It Matter?

A timestamp link is a regular YouTube URL with an extra parameter added to it that tells the player to start at a specific point in the video instead of from the beginning. For example, a link ending in &t=125s will open the video and jump straight to 2 minutes and 5 seconds in.

You can technically create these manually by right clicking the video player and choosing "Copy video URL at current time," but that only works while you are actively watching the video in the right spot. If you already know the exact minute and second you want to link to, typing it out by hand and getting the format right is easy to get wrong, especially when converting minutes and seconds into total seconds.

A dedicated generator removes that friction completely. You enter the video link and the time, and it builds the correctly formatted timestamp URL for you.

This matters more than it might seem for a simple reason: attention is limited. Research on video engagement, including data shared by YouTube's own Creator Academy, consistently shows that viewer drop off is highest in the first few seconds of a video. If you are sending someone a link expecting them to already be interested enough to wait through an intro, you are working against that drop off curve instead of with it. A timestamp link puts them exactly where you want them, immediately.

Additional Tips for Using Timestamp Links Effectively

  • Double check your timestamp before sharing. A link that opens a few seconds too early or too late can undercut the whole point. Preview the link once before sending it out widely.
  • Combine timestamp links with video chapters. If you are the video owner, adding YouTube chapters in the description alongside individual timestamp links gives viewers multiple ways to navigate.
  • Use timestamp links in email signatures or FAQs. If you get the same question often, link directly to the moment in a video where it is answered, rather than repeating the explanation every time.
  • Keep videos organized with clear, searchable titles. A timestamp link is only useful if people can trust the source video is still relevant and up to date. Rename outdated videos or add "updated" notes where needed.
  • Track which timestamp links get the most engagement. If you are sharing links across social media, keep an eye on which clips get the most clicks. It tells you what your audience actually cares about.
  • Do not overuse timestamps in a single message. Sending five different timestamp links in one email can overwhelm the reader. Pick the one moment that matters most for that specific conversation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a YouTube timestamp link?

A YouTube timestamp link is a video URL that includes a time parameter, causing the video to start playing at a specific point instead of from the beginning. It is commonly used to direct viewers straight to a relevant section of a longer video.

How do I create a YouTube timestamp link manually?

You can right click on a playing YouTube video and select "Copy video URL at current time," but this only works while actively watching the exact moment you want. A timestamp link generator lets you create the link for any moment without needing to watch the video first.

Do timestamp links work on mobile devices?

Yes. Timestamp links work across desktop browsers, the YouTube mobile app, and mobile browsers. The video will open and jump to the specified time regardless of the device being used.

Can I use a timestamp link for a private or unlisted YouTube video?

Yes, as long as the person opening the link has access to view the video. The timestamp parameter works the same way regardless of the video's privacy setting, but the viewer still needs proper access permissions.

Why should a business use timestamp links instead of sharing the full video?

Sharing a timestamp link removes friction for the viewer by taking them directly to the relevant moment, whether that is a product feature, a pricing explanation, or a customer testimonial. This increases the likelihood they engage with the specific content that matters for that conversation, rather than abandoning a long video partway through.
