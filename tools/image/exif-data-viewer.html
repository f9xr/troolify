EXIF Data Viewer: See What Hidden Metadata Your Photos Reveal

Every photo taken with a digital camera or smartphone can carry a hidden dossier: the camera and lens used, exact exposure settings, the moment it was taken and often the precise GPS coordinates of where you were standing. The Troolify EXIF Data Viewer decodes that hidden metadata straight from the raw file in your browser and presents it in a clean, categorized table - then lets you export a stripped, privacy-clean copy.

What is EXIF Data?

EXIF stands for Exchangeable Image File Format. When a camera snaps a picture it embeds a small data structure inside the JPEG, right next to the pixel data, in the APP1 segment followed by a TIFF header. That structure holds hundreds of possible tags: make and model, lens type, shutter speed, aperture, ISO, focal length, metering mode, capture date and GPS coordinates stored as rational numbers.

Our tool reads that binary structure directly - it locates the APP1 marker, parses the TIFF fields and converts the raw values into readable rows like f/2.8, 1/250 s, ISO 100 and 37.7749° N, 122.4194° W.

Why You Should Strip Metadata

Many platforms strip some EXIF when you upload, but many do not - and the files you share manually, from forum attachments to job applications, often keep every byte. That means your exact home address or workplace can be recovered from a holiday photo, and your device model can be used to profile you. Exporting a clean copy re-encodes the image through the canvas API, which discards all metadata by construction, so you can share with total freedom.

How to Use the EXIF Data Viewer

  1. Upload a photo - click the drop zone and pick a JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF or BMP from your device.
  2. Read the metadata - camera, lens, exposure, date and GPS rows appear instantly in categorized sections.
  3. Check the privacy chips - the tool flags when GPS coordinates or hidden tags are present.
  4. Export a clean copy - pick JPEG, PNG or WebP, choose quality, and download a file with all metadata removed.

What You Can Discover

  • Camera & lens - make, model, software, lens model and serial numbers.
  • Exposure - shutter speed, f-number, ISO, exposure program, metering mode and flash.
  • Timestamps - when the photo was taken, digitized and last modified, down to sub-seconds.
  • GPS - latitude, longitude and altitude, converted to decimal degrees for easy mapping.
  • Image details - pixel dimensions, orientation, colour space and resolution.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EXIF data?

EXIF is metadata that cameras and phones embed inside each photo. It records camera make and model, lens, exposure settings such as shutter speed, aperture and ISO, the capture date and time, and often the GPS coordinates of where the picture was taken.

Why should I strip EXIF data from my photos?

EXIF data can reveal your exact location, your device model and your habits whenever you share a photo. Stripping metadata before uploading to social media, forums or marketplaces protects your privacy and stops others recovering that hidden information.

Does this EXIF viewer upload my photos?

No. Your image is parsed entirely inside your browser tab using the File API and HTML canvas. The file is never uploaded anywhere, so photos containing precise GPS coordinates stay completely private.

Which image formats contain EXIF metadata?

JPEG is by far the most common format with EXIF data, written by almost every camera and phone. This viewer reads JPEG files directly, and also handles PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP images for file information and clean export.

Conclusion

Your photos know more about you than you might think. Use the EXIF Data Viewer to inspect exactly what is stored in your files, learn from the camera settings, then export a clean copy and share with confidence - all without a single image ever leaving your device.
