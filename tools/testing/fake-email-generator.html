Fake Email Generator: Synthetic Test Email Addresses on Demand

QA engineers, developers and data engineers need large batches of realistic-looking email addresses for form testing, database seeding and user flow validation. The Troolify Fake Email Generator creates synthetic emails across public, reserved and disposable domains, all generated locally in your browser.

What the Fake Email Generator Does

The tool combines a first name, a last name, a zero padded 4-digit suffix and a domain into one local part, so the address always parses as the domain it claims. Names are lowercased and the suffix is padded rather than drawn from random digits, which keeps every local part the same shape. You can restrict a run to public providers, to the RFC 2606 reserved domains, to disposable inboxes, or mix all three. Optional full name and domain fields let you control the output, and those switches drive every export. No data is uploaded anywhere.

How to Use the Fake Email Generator

  1. Set the count - enter how many emails to generate (1-100).
  2. Choose domain type - All mixed, public only, reserved only or disposable only.
  3. Toggle optional fields - enable full name and domain output as needed.
  4. Choose export format - Table, CSV, JSON or Plain text.
  5. Copy or download - grab the output instantly.

Use Cases

QA teams seed test databases with bulk email addresses. Developers validate email input fields and regex patterns. Marketing platforms test newsletter signup flows. Auth systems test password reset and verification email workflows with synthetic addresses.

An email on its own makes a thin fixture. Add a name, a phone and a country-correct address with the fake detail generator, and pull a matching address list from the random address generator when your form validates the two together. The tools catalog has the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which domain types are supported?

Public providers (gmail.com, outlook.com, yahoo.com, hotmail.com, icloud.com, proton.me), reserved domains (example.com, example.net, example.org), disposable inboxes (mailinator.com, guerrillamail.com, 10minutemail.com), or a mix of all three.

What does reserved mean?

RFC 2606 reserves example.com, example.net and example.org for documentation and testing. Mail to them is never delivered, so they are the right pick for docs, unit tests and CI fixtures. Public domains are real mailboxes and disposable ones self-destruct, so avoid both when a message has to be undeliverable on purpose.

Which fields appear in the export?

The email address is always included. The full name and the domain are separate columns you can switch off, and the same switches drive the table, the CSV, the JSON and the plain text download.

Is my data safe?

Generation runs in your browser with JavaScript. Nothing is uploaded, logged or stored on a server, and no address is ever contacted.

Conclusion

Whether you are testing a signup form, seeding a test database or validating email regex patterns, the Troolify Fake Email Generator gives you synthetic email addresses in seconds, entirely in your browser.
