Monthly website audit prompts that end in fixes

Most website audits end in a list. Somebody runs a scanner, exports 400 rows, and emails the file to a developer who cannot tell which 9 matter. The list becomes the deliverable, so almost nothing gets fixed.

A prompt changes the shape of that job. You describe the business, tick the checks, and the model returns findings sorted by impact with the exact change attached to each one. Same 15 checks every month, which means month 3 is directly comparable to month 1.

This generator builds that prompt in about a minute. Fill in the fields, choose a depth, copy the result into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity. Every Troolify prompt tool runs in your browser, and nothing you type here leaves the page.

Why a monthly cycle beats an annual one

Search results move every week. A competitor publishes 8 posts, a plugin update drops a noindex tag, a CDN change pushes LCP past its threshold. An annual audit catches the damage months after it started, by which point traffic has already moved.

Monthly checks catch the same class of problem while it is still cheap. Adding a missing title tag to one page takes 30 seconds. Adding it across 4,000 URLs after a template shipped badly takes a week and a rollback plan.

How to generate your audit prompt in 5 steps

Step 1: Add the site details

Paste the URL, brand, location and your money keywords. This block does the heaviest lifting in the whole prompt, because it tells the model which pages actually matter when it decides what counts as a high severity finding.

Step 2: Pick the checks

Run all 15 for a full monthly pass. For a quick weekly sanity check, keep 3, 5, 9 and 11. For a launch month, add 2 and 13, which is where redirect chains and templated duplication show up.

Step 3: Set depth and output shape

Quick scan gives you Critical and High items only. Standard is the practical monthly pass. Deep dive adds guideline citations and revenue estimates, and it costs more tokens, so save it for a quarterly review.

Step 4: Paste in your data

Most chat models cannot browse. Before you run the prompt, paste in a crawl export, your Search Console coverage report, or the rendered HTML of the key templates. Models with a research or agent mode attached can fetch pages themselves, so check what your tool can reach first.

Step 5: Copy, then check the work

Copy the prompt and run it. Verify every finding against your own data before you act on it. Models are good at spotting patterns in exports you paste in, and bad at knowing which of your templates the pattern actually affects.

Why severity ranking keeps reports honest

The single biggest failure mode in an AI audit is volume. A model that finds 200 issues has found you nothing. The generated prompt ranks by impact instead of by row count, using four fixed labels.

SeverityUse it whenExample
CriticalIt blocks indexation, breaks the conversion path, or exposes the siterobots.txt blocks the whole category folder
HighIt measurably weakens ranking, usability or measurementNoindex on the 12 highest-traffic money pages
MediumIt is suboptimal but carries no current penaltyMissing alt text on 40 low-traffic blog images
LowIt is a best-practice tidy-up with marginal direct effectInconsistent title tag capitalisation

That ordering matters for check 5 in particular. One missing title on a pricing page outranks 300 missing alt attributes, and any prompt that cannot tell you which is which will waste your week. What a monthly audit adds for AI search

Check 15 is the one most checklists skip. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini a question your customers ask, then see whether they cite you. Do it on the same 10 questions every month and you get a visibility trend line instead of a vibe.

Answer engines lean on structure. Clean schema, clear headings, a fast first paint and pages that state a fact plainly are what get quoted. The schema markup generator produces valid JSON-LD for LocalBusiness, Product, Article and FAQ blocks, which covers most of what check 11 looks for.

Key takeaways

  • Rank by impact: four fixed severity labels beat a 400 row export every month.
  • Keep the scope fixed: the same 15 checks each cycle is what makes trend lines readable.
  • Weight for your business: local, ecommerce and SaaS sites fail on completely different checks.
  • Paste your data in: most chat models cannot browse, so feed them your crawl and Search Console exports.
  • Verify before you ship: treat every finding as a lead that still needs a human check.
  • Keep an eye on AI search: the same 10 questions to 3 answer engines each month gives you a visibility number.

Frequently asked questions

What is a monthly website audit prompt?

It is a written set of instructions that tells an AI model which 15 recurring website checks to run, how to rank each finding by impact, and what a useful answer must contain: evidence, root cause, the exact fix and a way to verify it.

Which 15 checks should a monthly website audit run?

Image SEO, broken links and images, site speed and Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, on-page SEO and meta tags, content freshness, backlink profile, security and plugins, Search Console errors, local SEO and Google Business Profile, schema markup, analytics and conversion tracking, duplicate content, internal linking, and AI search visibility.

Do AI models actually crawl my website during the audit?

Most chat models cannot browse on their own. Paste in your exported crawl data, Search Console reports or rendered HTML first, then run the prompt. Some agents and research modes do have browsing tools attached, so check what your model can reach before you rely on it.

How long does a monthly website audit take?

The 15 checks add up to roughly 3 hours 45 minutes of manual work per month. With a ready prompt and a copied crawl export, you are looking at 30 to 60 minutes of review and sign off instead.

Will these prompts work on a WordPress site?

Yes. The generated prompt asks for evidence and exact fixes rather than plugin guesses, which is what you want on WordPress. Always confirm a fix in a staging copy first, and back up the database before any bulk change.

An audit that ends in a spreadsheet teaches you nothing you can act on. One that ends in 15 ranked findings, each with the change attached, is a work order you can hand to a developer and compare against next month.
