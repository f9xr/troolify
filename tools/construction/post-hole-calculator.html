Post Hole Calculator

Setting a post in concrete rarely needs a full hole of mix - the post itself takes up part of the volume. This calculator computes the hole volume, subtracts the volume of the round or square post, and returns the real concrete volume per hole and for the whole run.

How to Use the Post Hole Calculator

  1. Enter the hole diameter - round holes; for square holes measure across.
  2. Enter the depth - below frost line if supported by a structure.
  3. Pick the post - square (4x4, 6x6) or round.
  4. Set post count - one per hole.

Why the Estimate Matters

A standard fence post hole - 10 inches diameter by 36 deep - around a 4x4 post takes about 3 bags of 80 lb concrete, and a typical 10-post run lands near 30 bags. The calculator reproduces that math from your exact dimensions so signs, mailboxes and deck posts all get sized honestly.

Contractor Tip

Keep 2-3 inches of the post buried purely in concrete but keep the concrete above surrounding grade slightly sloped - it sheds water away from the post and doubles its life.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of concrete per fence post?

A 10 in x 36 in hole with a 4x4 post needs about 2.9 bags of 80 lb concrete.

Should the hole be round or square?

Round is easier to dig and uses less concrete for the same holding power; square holes are fine but compute as the enclosing circle.

Conclusion

From mailbox posts to fence runs, the Post Hole Calculator returns the exact concrete per hole and in total - locally in your browser.
