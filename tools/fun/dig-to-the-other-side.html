Dig to the Other Side Calculator

Every point on Earth has exactly one antipode - the spot you would reach by drilling straight through the planet. The Dig to the Other Side Calculator takes your coordinates and answers with the Tasta Space hub's physics: where you emerge, whether that is land or water, the nearest coast if it is ocean, how long the fall takes through a uniform Earth and through the real layered Earth, and the top speed you would hit at the centre.

How to Use

  1. Enter latitude and longitude - or tap a preset city, or press Use my location.
  2. Click Dig! - the exit point, verdict and physics appear instantly.
  3. Read the verdict - it tells you whether to bring a boat, and how far the nearest shore is.

Why It Matters

About 71% of the antipodal land question ends in water: most places on land come out in the ocean. The tool also shows the real fall time - roughly 38 to 42 minutes - and stacks it against the Kola Superdeep Borehole, the deepest hole humans have ever made.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an antipode?

The antipode is the point diametrically opposite yours on the globe: same distance from the centre, opposite side. Latitude flips sign; longitude is mirrored through the poles.

Why do so many antipodes land in the ocean?

Because the continents are unevenly spread - most land sits in the northern hemisphere, whose antipodes fall in the southern oceans. Only a few places, like parts of Spain and New Zealand, meet land on the other side.

How exact are the fall times?

Two models are shown: a uniform-density Earth (about 42 minutes, a clean physics result) and the real layered Earth from seismic data (about 38 minutes, per Klotz, American Journal of Physics, 2015).

Is this real physics or a joke?

Both, deliberately. The numbers are real; the boat advice is genuine too - most tunnels end in salt water.

Conclusion

One shaft, one planet, one exit - find your antipode and pack accordingly.
