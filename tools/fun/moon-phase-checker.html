Moon Phase Checker

The Moon Phase Checker computes the lunar phase for any date using the Tasta Space hub, which follows Jean Meeus' Astronomical Algorithms. Alongside the phase name it draws the exact lit shape, gives the illumination percentage, the Moon's age in the current cycle, its distance, and the dates of the next new, first-quarter, full and last-quarter moons. The tool loads tonight's Moon as soon as you open the page.

How to Use

  1. Pick a date - defaults to today; Today jumps back to now.
  2. Choose a hemisphere - waxing and waning are mirrored between north and south, and so is the drawing.
  3. Click Check the Moon - the phase drawing, stats and next phase dates appear together.

Why It Matters

Photographers, gardeners, anglers and night-shift walkers all plan around the Moon. One glance tells you whether tonight is bright enough to walk without a lamp - or dark enough for deep-sky observing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the phase?

The hub computes phases with the Meeus algorithms (chapters 47 to 49), accurate to well under a minute for phase times - far finer than the daily view shown here.

Why does the hemisphere matter?

From the southern hemisphere the Moon is flipped: a waxing gibbous that leans right in the north leans left in the south. Switch hemispheres to match what you actually see overhead.

What are full moon names?

Folklore names such as Harvest Moon or Wolf Moon are shown when the chosen date is close to a full moon and the hub knows the name for that lunation.

What do Blue moon and Supermoon mean?

A Blue moon is the second full moon in a calendar month; a Supermoon is a full moon near perigee, when the disc appears slightly larger and brighter. Both flags appear on the result when they apply.
