Macro Calculator: A Numbers-First Nutrition Plan

"Eat less, move more" is a slogan, not a plan. A macro calculator turns your calorie goal into g of protein, carbohydrates and fat - numbers you can actually log in a food diary and hit daily.

Protein, Carbs and Fat at a Glance

Protein rebuilds muscle and keeps you full; carbs power training and thinking; fat supports hormones. Protein and carbs carry 4 kcal per gram, fat carries 9 - so the macro grams you see always add back up to your calorie target.

How the Targets Are Built

The calculator starts from your TDEE (Mifflin-St Jeor BMR x activity), applies your goal (-20% for a cut, +15% for a bulk), then sets protein by your weight and goal - roughly 1.6-2.2 g per kg. Fat takes 25% of the remaining calories and carbs fill the rest. A sensible, sustainable split for most people.

Eating to Your Numbers

Spread the protein across 3-4 meals for satiety and better training recovery. There is no perfect meal timing - consistency with the totals matters far more. If cutting, hold protein at the higher end to protect the muscle you have worked for, using the Protein Intake Calculator to double-check your numbers.

Calories First, Macros Second

Macros matter - but only after calories. Lock in your daily calorie need first, then let this tool divide it into the three nutrients. Weight tracking over a couple of weeks tells you if the plan needs adjusting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are macros?

Macros are the three energy nutrients - protein (4 kcal/g), carbohydrates (4 kcal/g) and fat (9 kcal/g). Hitting a calorie target is the same as hitting a macro combination.

How much protein should I eat?

1.6-2.2 g per kg of body weight per day protects muscle, with the higher end during a calorie deficit. This calculator scales protein with your goal.

Do macros matter for weight loss?

Calories decide whether you lose weight; macros decide how. A higher-protein split preserves lean mass and keeps you fuller, making the deficit easier to sustain.

Is a calorie always a calorie?

For total energy balance, yes. But protein satiates, carbs fuel training and fat is calorie-dense, so balance the split rather than chasing extremes.

Conclusion

Get your calorie goal, split it into macros, log it consistently, weigh yourself weekly - and adjust. That cycle is the whole nutrition game, and this calculator gives you round one in seconds.
