Calorie Calculator: Turn a Number Into a Weight Plan

Weight management is calories in versus calories out. The hard part is knowing what "calories out" actually is for you. This calorie calculator estimates it from your height, age, sex, weight and activity - then translates it into practical daily targets for maintaining, losing or gaining weight.

How the Calorie Calculator Works

The tool first estimates your basal metabolic rate with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the same formula used by our BMR Calculator. It then multiplies your BMR by an activity factor - from 1.2 for a sedentary day to 1.9 for hard training - to find your maintenance calories (TDEE).

From there the targets are simple:

  • Maintenance - eat exactly your TDEE to hold weight steady.
  • Weight loss - subtract 500 kcal (about 0.5 kg per week) or 750 kcal (about 0.7 kg per week).
  • Weight gain - add 500 kcal for a steady surplus.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Enter your age, sex, height and weight.
  2. Pick the activity level that best matches your typical week.
  3. Read your targets instantly - maintenance, two loss options and a gain option.

All results update live and are computed locally in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

What a Deficit of 500 or 750 Calories Means

Roughly 3,500 calories equal one pound (0.45 kg) of body fat, so a 500 calorie daily deficit compound to about half a kilogram per week. That is a safe, sustainable pace for most people. The 750 calorie option accelerates progress to about three-quarter kilograms per week but demands more consistency, so it is best suited to those who find the lighter deficit too slow.

Making the Numbers Work Day to Day

Pair the target with volume-based tracking rather than weighing food to the gram - most people stay consistent when the plan feels flexible. Check your weight trend weekly rather than daily, and adjust by 100-200 calories if the scale stalls for more than two weeks.

For the full picture, combine this tool with the Ideal Weight Calculator (to set a realistic endpoint) and the Water Intake Calculator (to stay hydrated while changing your intake). You can also verify your body category with the BMI Calculator.

Once your calorie target is set, split it into protein, fat and carbs with the Macro Calculator, and credit your daily movement with the Steps to Calories Calculator.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many calories should I eat to maintain my weight?

Your maintenance calories equal your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE): your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. This calculator estimates BMR with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and multiplies it by the activity level you select.

Is a 500 calorie deficit enough to lose weight?

Yes. A 500 calorie daily deficit equates to roughly one pound (0.45 kg) of fat loss per week, which is a safe, sustainable rate for most people. A 750 calorie deficit speeds this up to about 1.5 pounds per week but requires more discipline.

How does this calorie calculator work?

It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate your BMR, then multiplies by an activity factor (1.2 to 1.9) to get maintenance calories. Weight-loss and gain targets are then calculated as fixed deficits or surpluses from that number.

Should I eat back calories burned through exercise?

If your activity level already reflects your workout routine, no - eating back exercise calories can cancel your deficit. If you are unusually active on top of your chosen level, a small topping-up is reasonable. Monitor your weight trend and adjust.

Conclusion

An estimate is better than a guess. Use this calorie calculator to anchor your daily intake, track your weight over a few weeks, and adjust gradually from a plan that is personal to you.
