Body Fat Percentage: Know What the Scale Can't Tell You

Your weight alone cannot tell you whether the change you see is muscle or fat. Body composition can - and the US Navy circumference method puts an accurate-enough estimate on it using nothing but a tape measure.

What the US Navy Method Measures

The method uses height, neck and waist circumferences - plus hip and weight for women - in standard logarithmic equations. Fat stored around the middle is the biggest metabolic risk, so waist and hip readings carry the most weight in the estimate.

How to Take the Measurements

  1. Height - stand straight, no shoes.
  2. Neck - just below the larynx (Adam's apple) with the tape sloping slightly down the front.
  3. Waist - at navel level for men; at the narrowest point for women, tape parallel to the floor.
  4. Hip (women) - at the widest point over the buttocks.

Pull the tape snug but not tight, take each reading twice and average if they differ.

Interpreting Your Result

  • Men - 14-17% is "fit", 18-24% average, 25%+ higher risk. Below 6-13% is competitive-athlete territory.
  • Women - 21-24% fit, 25-31% average, 32%+ higher risk. Below 14-20% is athlete territory.

Essential fat floors (3-5% men, 10-13% women) keep basic physiology working - do not chase a number below them.

BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, which is why a heavy athlete can look "overweight" on the scale. Body fat percentage fills that gap. Use both together - start with the BMI Calculator for a quick screen and refine with body fat here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the US Navy body fat formula?

The US Navy method estimates body fat from tape measurements - height, neck and waist, plus hip for women - using standard logarithmic equations. It is a quick, inexpensive estimate that correlates well with more advanced methods.

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

For men, 14-17% is fit and 18-24% average; for women, 21-24% is fit and 25-31% average. Athletes often sit lower (6-13% men, 14-20% women). Essential fat floors are about 3-5% for men and 10-13% for women.

How accurate is this calculator?

Circumference methods are accurate to roughly +/- 3-4% for most people when measured carefully, and are excellent for tracking change over time. DEXA scans remain the most precise reference.

Why do I need my waist measurement for body fat?

Abdominal fat is the strongest predictor of metabolic risk. Waist and hip circumferences let the equations infer fat distribution - information BMI cannot provide.

Conclusion

Circumference measurements cost nothing, take three minutes and give you a body composition trend worth tracking month to month. Calculate yours now, note the date, and re-measure in a few weeks to see real progress.
