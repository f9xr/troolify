Heart Rate Zones: Train With Intention, Not Guesswork

Eighty percent of your easy runs being too hard is the most common training mistake there is. Heart rate zones remove the guesswork by telling you precisely how hard your heart is working - and this calculator builds your personal zones from two simple numbers.

Estimating Your Max Heart Rate

Most athletes start from 220 minus age, until a real test refines the number. The Tanaka formula (208 - 0.7 x age) often fits better for older athletes. Either way, treat the result as a working estimate, not a verdict.

The Five Training Zones

  • Zone 1 (50-60%) - recovery. Walking, cool-downs.
  • Zone 2 (60-70%) - endurance. Your aerobic engine, most weekly volume lives here.
  • Zone 3 (70-80%) - tempo. Brisk but sustainable.
  • Zone 4 (80-90%) - threshold. Hard intervals.
  • Zone 5 (90-100%) - max effort. Sprint work, short bursts.

Karvonen: Zones That Fit Your Fitness

Simple percentages treat a beginner and a marathoner the same. The Karvonen method uses your heart rate reserve - max minus resting - and adds resting HR back, so a fitter heart (lower resting HR) gets proportionally narrower, more accurate zones. This calculator uses it automatically when a resting HR is entered.

How to Use Your Zones

Spend most of your running or riding in zone 2, sprinkle tempo and threshold work in once or twice a week, and keep zone 5 for short sprints. Pair zone work with the Steps to Calories Calculator to see what your sessions actually cost, and use the Calorie Calculator to fuel them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my max heart rate?

The fastest your heart can beat at maximum effort (bpm), commonly estimated as 220 minus your age, with 208 - 0.7 x age (Tanaka) as a refinement. A laboratory stress test is the only exact measurement.

What is the Karvonen formula?

Karvonen targets = resting HR + (desired %) x (max HR - resting HR). Because it uses your heart rate reserve, zones adapt automatically to your fitness level.

Which heart rate zone should I train in?

Zone 2 (60-70%) builds the aerobic base for most steady training; zones 4-5 serve intervals and speed. Beginners should live in zone 2 first.

Is a heart rate calculator medical advice?

No. Zone estimates are general training guidance. Get medical clearance first if you have heart conditions, take heart-rate-affecting medication, or are new to exercise.

Conclusion

Knowing your zones turns every session into a deliberate stimulus instead of a random effort. Get your numbers, wear the monitor, and watch your easy days get genuinely easy.
