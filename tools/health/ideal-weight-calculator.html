Ideal Weight Calculator: What Is a Healthy Weight For You?

"What should I weigh?" is one of the most common health questions, yet there is no single perfect answer. This calculator gives you three useful perspectives on your height: the Devine formula, the Miller formula, and the more evidence-backed healthy BMI range.

What "Ideal Weight" Actually Means

Ideals vary because formula creators started from different assumptions. The Devine formula was built in 1974 for drug-dosing calculations, and the Miller formula in 1983 revised it for a more realistic population sample. Both express weight as a base for a 5-foot person plus extra weight per inch of height, and they differ slightly between men and women because of typical differences in body composition.

You will see both results from this tool - disagreement between formulas is normal and expected.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Enter your height in centimetres (the tool converts automatically).
  2. Select your sex.
  3. Read your results - Devine ideal, Miller ideal and healthy BMI range, each in kg and lb.

Why the Healthy BMI Range Is the Better Target

Unlike single-point formulas, a healthy BMI range of 18.5-24.9 kg/m² is derived from population studies linking weight to health outcomes. For your height it translates into a range of acceptable weights rather than one magic number. Most guidance recommends aiming anywhere inside that band, weighted toward realistic body-composition goals.

Putting the Numbers to Work

Set a goal inside your healthy range, then build a plan. Follow the BMI Calculator to track your position within the band, and use the Calorie Calculator to find the daily intake that moves you steadily toward it. Muscle mass, frame size and age all shift what feels right for an individual, so treat the numbers as guidance rather than a verdict.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Devine formula for ideal weight?

The Devine formula gives men a base ideal weight of 50 kg plus 2.3 kg for every inch of height over 5 feet, and women a base of 45.5 kg plus 2.3 kg per inch over 5 feet. It was originally developed in 1974 for drug-dosing calculations but is widely reused for healthy weight estimation.

What is the Miller formula for ideal weight?

The Miller formula (1983) gives men a base of 56.2 kg plus 1.41 kg per inch over 5 feet, and women a base of 53.1 kg plus 1.36 kg per inch over 5 feet. It tends to produce slightly different results from Devine, so comparing both is useful.

Is there a single perfect weight for my height?

No. Ideal weight formulas each reflect different historical assumptions, and no single number captures health for a whole person. The healthy BMI range (a body mass index of 18.5 to 24.9) is the most evidence-backed target to aim for.

What is a healthy BMI range?

The World Health Organization defines a healthy (normal) BMI as 18.5 to 24.9 kg/m2 for adults. Multiply both endpoints by your height in meters squared to get the healthy weight range for your specific height.

Conclusion

No formula is the final word on your ideal weight. Use the Devine and Miller estimates as reference points and the healthy BMI range as your guide, then build a sustainable plan around a goal you can maintain.
