TDEE: The One Number Your Nutrition Starts From

Every nutrition plan begins with one question: how many calories do you burn in a day? That number is your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), and it decides whether your meals hit a deficit, a surplus or maintenance. This calculator works it out with the widely used Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

How TDEE Is Calculated

First, your Basal Metabolic Rate - the calories your body burns at complete rest - is estimated from age, sex, height and weight. That baseline is multiplied by an activity factor (1.2 sedentary up to 1.9 for hard training) to account for movement, fidgeting, digestion and exercise.

Maintenance, Cutting and Bulking

  • Maintenance - eat your TDEE and stay the same weight.
  • Cut - a 250-500 kcal daily deficit loses roughly 0.25-0.5 kg per week, sparing muscle.
  • Bulk - a 250-500 kcal surplus supports slow, lean gains.

Pair your target with the Macro Calculator to split the calories into protein, carbs and fat, and use the Protein Intake Calculator to protect muscle during a cut.

Track, Don't Guess

Formulas land within about +/- 15% for most people. Track your weight for 1-2 weeks at the suggested intake, then adjust in ~100-150 kcal steps until the scale moves the way you want.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure is the calories your body burns in a full day, including basal metabolism, digestion and physical activity. Eating at TDEE maintains weight.

How do I lose or gain weight with TDEE?

A ~500 kcal daily deficit averages roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week; a similar surplus drives a slow lean gain. The calculator shows both targets from your TDEE.

Which activity level should I choose?

Reflect your average week honestly. Sedentary is desk work, light is 1-3 training days, moderate is 3-5, very active is daily hard training, and extra active adds a physical job. When in doubt, choose the lower option.

Is the TDEE estimate accurate?

TDEE formulas are accurate to roughly +/- 15% for most people. Use the number as a starting point, track your weight and adjust in ~100 kcal steps.

Conclusion

Your TDEE is the anchor for every calorie-based goal. Get yours, set a sensible cut or bulk, and let the number adjust itself as your weight and activity change.
