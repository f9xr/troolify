Lean Body Mass: Watch the Signal Under the Noise

Your scale reports everything - water, muscle, bone, food in transit. Lean body mass filters out the fat, leaving a number that actually responds to training and diet. This tool finds yours from height and weight, then sharpens the estimate if you know your body fat.

What LBM Includes

Lean body mass is literally non-fat body weight: skeletal muscle, bone, organs, fluid, connective tissue and skin. It's why two people at the same weight can look completely different - the mix of lean versus fat is what your eye sees.

The Boer Formula

For men, Boer predicts LBM = 0.407 x weight (kg) + 0.267 x height (cm) - 19.2. For women: 0.252 x weight + 0.473 x height - 48.3. It lands within a few percent of a DEXA scan for most people and needs nothing but a tape measure and scale.

Why Track LBM Instead of Weight

  • Cutting - if LBM holds steady while weight falls, your muscle is safe.
  • Bulk cycles - rising LBM means real tissue, not just fat.
  • Protein goals - LBM is a more precise base than total weight for your protein target.

Pair it with the body fat percentage calculator to get both sides of the equation in one sitting, and run the macros off the result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is lean body mass?

Everything in your body that isn't fat - muscle, bone, organs, fluids and skin. It's a better basis than total weight for protein targets and tracking composition changes.

What is a good lean body mass percentage?

Most healthy adults are 65-80% lean (20-35% body fat); athletes run higher. Track your own LBM over time instead of chasing a universal number.

How accurate is the Boer formula?

Within a few percent of DEXA for most people. As a height-and-weight estimate it is less reliable at body-fat extremes, where entering a real reading improves accuracy.

Why should I track LBM instead of weight?

The scale can't separate fat from muscle. Flat LBM during a cut means your muscle is safe; rising LBM during a bulk means real gain.

Conclusion

Stop optimizing the number on the scale and start tracking the tissue that does the work. A monthly Boer reading - taken at the same time of day - is a free body-composition upgrade.
