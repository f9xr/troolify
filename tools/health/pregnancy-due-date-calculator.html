Pregnancy Due Date: How It's Worked Out

Your "due date" is really a best guess: the day your baby is statistically most likely to arrive, calculated from 266 days of actual pregnancy plus two weeks of pre-ovulation. Only about 5% of babies appear on the exact date - mine included, apparently.

Naegele's Rule and the 280-Day Count

Gently touch your LMP's month and add 280 days. That's Naegele's rule. It assumes a 28-day cycle, so our tool shifts the estimate by the difference when your cycles run longer or shorter - a 35-day cycle pushes the EDD about a week out.

What Your Trimester Means

  • First trimester (0-13 weeks) - rapid organ development, so medications and lifestyle matter most.
  • Second trimester (14-27 weeks) - growth and movement; many people feel best here.
  • Third trimester (28-40 weeks) - the baby gains weight fast; birth plans come into focus.

Whether you measure from LMP or conception, the dates line up on the same due date. Pair this page with the ovulation calculator to understand the fertility window that started it all.

Why It's an Estimate

Cycle lengths vary, ovulation shifts, and every pregnancy is different. The early ultrasound date remains the gold standard; use this tool as a planning aid and let your provider set the official date.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a due date calculated?

Add 280 days to the first day of your last menstrual period and adjust for cycle length, or add 266 days to your conception date. Only about 5% of babies arrive exactly on the date - it's an estimate.

Which trimester am I in?

First trimester is weeks 0-13, second is weeks 14-27, and third runs from week 28 to delivery. The tool shows your trimester automatically.

Does cycle length change my due date?

Yes. Naegele's rule assumes 28 days. Offset the due date by the difference: a 35-day cycle adds about 7 days; a 24-day cycle subtracts about 4.

Is this a substitute for my ultrasound?

No. A dating ultrasound is more accurate than date-based math. Confirm your official due date with your provider.

Conclusion

Work from the date your provider confirms, keep the other 95% of babies in mind, and pack the hospital bag a bit early.
