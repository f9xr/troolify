Waist to Hip Ratio: Where Fat Lives Matters

Two identical people can carry the same weight in completely different places - and that difference changes health risk. Your waist-to-hip ratio (WHR) captures where your fat sits in one simple number.

Why Fat Distribution Counts

Belly fat drains directly into the liver and is far more metabolically active than the fat stored around your hips, so "apple-shaped" bodies face more cardiovascular and diabetes risk at the same BMI. WHR is the fastest way to tell your body shape apart from the top of your BMI.

WHR Categories (WHO)

  • Men: below 0.90 is low risk; 0.90-0.99 moderate; 1.00+ high.
  • Women: below 0.80 is low risk; 0.80-0.84 moderate; 0.85+ high.

Measure at the right spots: waist between the bottom rib and the top of the hip bone, relaxed after breathing out; hips over the widest point of the buttocks.

Improving the Number

Central fat responds well to a calorie deficit and to strength training. Track your WHR alongside the BMI calculator and the body fat percentage calculator to watch progress from three angles, and pair training with the steps to calories tracker.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy waist-to-hip ratio?

Low risk is below 0.90 for men and below 0.85 for women. Above 1.0 for men and above 0.85 for women points to increasing risk from central fat.

Why is waist-to-hip ratio important?

Central fat is more metabolically active than hip fat, so it raises heart-disease and diabetes risk. WHR measures distribution; BMI only measures overall size.

How do I measure correctly?

Waist: mid-point between the bottom rib and hip bone, relaxed. Hips: over the widest part of the buttocks. Keep the tape level and use the same unit for both.

Is WHR better than BMI?

They complement each other. A normal-weight person with a high WHR can still carry risky central fat, so check both.

Conclusion

Your WHR is a free, tape-measure screening you can repeat at home every few weeks. Combined with BMI, it paints a far truer picture than the scale alone.
