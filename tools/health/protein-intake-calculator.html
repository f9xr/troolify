Protein Intake: The Simplest Lever in Your Diet

Of everything you track, protein deserves the most attention. It builds the muscle you train for, keeps you full, and protects lean mass when you eat in a deficit. This calculator turns your body weight into a clear gram target - no app required.

Why Protein Scales With Body Weight

Muscle mass scales with body weight, so protein targets are expressed per kilogram. Basic maintenance starts at 0.8 g/kg. Training adults want 1.6-2.2 g/kg, and people cutting should sit near the top of that range to hold onto muscle while losing fat.

Practical Ways to Hit the Number

A palm-sized serving of chicken, fish or tofu is roughly 25-30 g. Spread servings across the meals you actually eat so each one carries a fair share - the per-meal split in this tool does that math for you. Whole foods first; shakes are a convenient top-up, not a replacement.

Protein Inside Your Macro Plan

Protein provides 4 kcal per gram, so it occupies a slice of your macro targets. Set the calorie level first, fix protein next, then let carbs and fat fill the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein do I need per day?

Basic maintenance is about 0.8 g per kg of body weight. Active adults typically need 1.6-2.2 g/kg, higher during a deficit or heavy training.

Is too much protein harmful?

For healthy organs, moderate-to-high protein intakes are generally safe. Going far beyond 2.5 g/kg adds little benefit and costs calories better spent elsewhere.

When should I eat protein?

Spread it across 3-5 meals. A 20-40 g serving after training supports recovery, and protein at breakfast improves daily fullness.

What protein do I need while losing weight?

Plenty - around 1.8-2.2 g/kg. Protein preserves lean mass in a deficit and increases satiety, making the calorie target easier to stick to.

Conclusion

Set the number, log it for a week, and you will quickly learn which meals need a boost. Consistency beats perfection - the target only works if you hit most of it most days.
