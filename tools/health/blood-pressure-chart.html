Blood Pressure Chart: Classify Your Reading

Blood pressure is written as systolic over diastolic. This blood pressure chart classifies a reading into standard categories so you can see where it falls at a glance.

What the two numbers mean

Systolic is the pressure when the heart contracts; diastolic is the pressure between beats. Both matter, and the higher category of the two determines your classification.

The categories

Readings move from normal through elevated, stage 1 and stage 2 hypertension to hypertensive crisis. A single high reading is not a diagnosis; consistent patterns across time are what clinicians assess.

How to use Blood Pressure Chart

  1. Enter your systolic reading.
  2. Enter your diastolic reading.
  3. Read your category and compare against the reference table.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Measure while rested and seated.
  • Take two readings and average them.
  • A single reading is a snapshot, not a diagnosis.

Key takeaways

  • The higher of the two numbers sets the category.
  • Consistent high readings warrant medical advice.
  • Crisis readings need urgent care.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal reading?

Below 120 over 80 is considered normal.

Is one high reading serious?

Not necessarily; sustained elevations matter more.

Should I stop medication?

Never change treatment without your clinician.
