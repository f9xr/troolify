Standard Deviation Calculator: Measure the Spread of Your Data

An average tells you the middle of your data, but two datasets can share the same mean and be wildly different in character. Standard deviation measures how spread out the values are - the single most useful companion to the mean, and the foundation of everything from quality control to grading curves.

What Standard Deviation Tells You

In essence, standard deviation answers "how far from the average do values typically sit?" A small deviation means values cluster tightly around the mean - consistent exam scores, reliable production runs. A large deviation means big swings between values - volatile stock returns, uneven class performance.

The formula is: variance = sum of (x - mean)² divided by n (or n-1 for samples), and standard deviation is the square root of that variance.

Population vs Sample: When to Use n and n-1

The tool reports both versions so you never have to guess:

  • Population (n) - use when your list contains every member of the group, such as the heights of every student in one class.
  • Sample (n-1) - use when your values are a subset of a larger group, such as a random sample of a city's households. Dividing by n-1 gives an unbiased estimate of the true population variance.

For anything the rest of the world treats as data from a larger population - almost all survey and research work - the sample version is the one to quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between population and sample standard deviation?

Population standard deviation is used when your list contains every member of the group you study and divides by n. Sample standard deviation is used when your list is a subset of a larger group and divides by n-1 to correct for the extra uncertainty. Most real-world analysis uses the sample version.

When should I divide by n vs n-1?

Divide by n (population) when you have all values of the entire population, such as every student's score in one fixed class. Divide by n-1 (sample) when you only have a subset drawn from a larger population, such as a random sample of voters - n-1 gives an unbiased estimate of the true variance.

What is the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance is the average squared distance of each value from the mean, so it is in squared units. Standard deviation is the square root of variance, which brings the number back into the original units - such as pounds, exam points or seconds - making it much easier to interpret.

How do I enter a list of numbers?

Paste or type your numbers separated by commas, spaces or new lines - for example '4, 8, 6, 5, 3' or one number per line. Use a period (dot) as the decimal separator; commas always act as item separators.
