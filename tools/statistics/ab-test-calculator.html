A/B Test Calculator: Was the Difference Real?

Variant B converted better than variant A - but would it again, or did you just get lucky? This A/B test calculator compares two conversion rates with a two-proportion z-test and reports the relative uplift, the z-score, a two-tailed p-value, and a plain verdict at 95% confidence.

How significance is judged

Each variant has a conversion rate: conversions over visitors. The test asks whether the gap between those two rates is larger than the random noise you would expect from sampling. It pools the two rates to estimate a common baseline, computes the standard error of the difference, and divides the observed difference by that error to get a z-score.

From the z-score the tool derives a two-tailed p-value. When the p-value is below 0.05, the difference is called statistically significant at the 95% level, meaning there is less than a one in twenty chance of seeing a gap this big if the two variants were truly identical.

What significance does not tell you

Significance is not the same as importance. A tiny, real difference can be significant with a huge sample, and a large difference can fail to reach significance with a small one. The test also assumes visitors were split randomly and independently, that each visitor is counted once, and that the conversion measure is the same for both groups.

Decide in advance how long the test will run and what counts as a conversion, then resist the urge to peek and stop the moment you see a winner - that inflates false positives.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Do not stop a test the moment it looks significant; that biases the result.
  • The bigger the effect you want to detect, the smaller the sample you need.
  • Keep everything except the change under test identical between variants, or you will not know what caused the shift.

Key takeaways

  • A p-value below 0.05 is the conventional bar for significance at 95%.
  • Significant does not automatically mean important or profitable.
  • Random, independent assignment is the assumption that makes the test valid.

Frequently asked questions

What does the p-value mean?

It is the probability of seeing a difference at least as large as the one you observed if the two variants were actually identical. Smaller means stronger evidence of a real difference.

What is the z-score?

It measures how many standard errors the observed difference is away from zero. Larger magnitudes point to a more certain difference.

Is 95% confidence always the right threshold?

It is the common default. For higher-stakes decisions a stricter level such as 99% may be appropriate.

Can I use this with small samples?

The z-test is approximate and least reliable with small counts. With very few conversions, treat the result as a hint rather than proof.

Is my data uploaded?

No. All calculation happens locally in your browser.
