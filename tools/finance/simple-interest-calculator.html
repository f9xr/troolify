Simple Interest Calculator: One Formula, Any Direction

Simple interest is the most transparent way money is priced: a fixed percentage of your principal, every year, with no snowballing. This calculator lets you use the formula forwards (find the interest and total amount) or backwards (find the rate or the time).

The Simple Interest Formula

Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

with the rate as a decimal and time in years. The total amount is simply principal + interest. Because the rate is steady and the principal never grows, the math stays linear and easy to check by hand.

How to Use the Simple Interest Calculator

  1. Calculate interest - enter principal, rate and time to get the interest and the total amount.
  2. Find rate - if you know the interest earned, enter principal, time and interest to solve the rate.
  3. Find time - enter principal, rate and interest to see how many years it takes.

In the Calculate mode, the time field accepts decimals and fractions of a year (for example 0.5 for six months), so short loans and deposits work exactly as well as long ones.

Simple vs Compound Interest

Simple interest suits short-term loans and bonds. For anything spanning years, compounding changes the picture dramatically - that is why long-term savings are quoted with compounding. Compare both before you decide: use this calculator for the linear view and our Compound Interest Calculator for the grow-on-earnings view.

Where Simple Interest Shows Up

Vehicle loans, personal loans and payday lending often use simple interest, as do many bonds and most bank interest calculations on short terms. When you lend to a friend, this is also the honest way to price the favor - principal, rate and time, nothing hidden. Frequently Asked Questions

What is simple interest?

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the original principal, never on previously earned interest. The formula is Interest = Principal × Rate × Time, and it is common for short-term loans, bonds and promissory notes.

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is charged only on the original principal, so growth is linear. Compound interest adds each period's interest to the balance so growth accelerates. Over long periods compound interest produces dramatically larger totals.

How do I find the interest rate or time in simple interest?

Rearrange the formula: Rate = Interest / (Principal × Time) and Time = Interest / (Principal × Rate). The calculator solves for the unknown automatically in the Find rate and Find time modes.
