Loan Payment Calculator: Know Your Real Cost Before You Borrow

Loans are quoted in interest rates and terms, but what you actually pay is a monthly figure plus interest on top. This calculator turns a loan amount, rate and term into the numbers that matter: your monthly payment, total interest, and the real total cost of borrowing.

How Loan Payments Are Calculated

Most consumer loans use the standard amortization formula:

Monthly payment = P × (r/12) / (1 - (1 + r/12)-n)

where P is the loan amount, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, and n is the total number of monthly payments. Each payment covers the interest that has accrued since the last payment; the remainder reduces the principal.

How to Use the Loan Payment Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount you plan to borrow.
  2. Add the annual interest rate quoted by the lender.
  3. Choose the term in years, or switch to fixed-payment mode and enter the monthly amount you can afford.

The results show your monthly payment, total interest, total cost, and - in fixed-payment mode - how many months it takes to be debt-free.

Why Total Cost Matters More Than the Rate

A slightly lower rate can save thousands over a long term, but extending the term to shrink the payment usually raises the total interest paid. Compare totals rather than just monthly amounts, and pair this tool with our Simple Interest Calculator for quick interest checks, or our Compound Interest Calculator to see the same math working for your savings instead.

Paying Off Your Loan Faster

Because interest accrues on the outstanding balance, every extra dollar you pay early saves interest later. Use fixed-payment mode to see how an additional amount each month shortens the term and trims the total cost. Even a modest increase makes a big difference across several years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is my monthly loan payment calculated?

The standard amortization formula is M = P × (r/12) / (1 - (1 + r/12)^(-n)), where P is the loan amount, r is the annual rate as a decimal, and n is the number of monthly payments. Each payment covers accrued interest first, with the remainder reducing the principal.

What is an amortizing loan?

An amortizing loan is repaid in equal installments over a fixed term. Early payments are mostly interest; as the principal shrinks, a larger share of each payment goes to the balance until the loan is fully paid off.

How can I pay off my loan faster?

Paying above your required monthly amount goes straight to the principal, cutting the remaining interest. The fixed-payment mode shows exactly how a higher monthly payment shortens your term and lowers the total cost.
