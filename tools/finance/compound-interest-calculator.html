User Guide

What is compound interest?
Compound interest is interest earned on your original money and on the interest it has already earned. The longer you stay invested, the faster your balance grows.
How to use this tool
  • Initial Investment: the amount you start with.
  • Contribution: money you add regularly, and how often. Choose whether it's added at the start or end of each period.
  • Annual Rate: the yearly return you expect, before inflation.
  • Compounding: how often interest is added to your balance.
  • Years: how long the money stays invested (1–100).
  • Inflation: used to show what the future amount would be worth in today's money.
  • Results update automatically as you type. Reset restores the example values; Clear empties every field; Copy result puts the figures on your clipboard.
Understanding your results
  • Future Value: your total balance at the end (the highlighted box).
  • Total Contributed: everything you put in yourself.
  • Interest Earned: the growth generated by compounding.
  • Real Value: the future value adjusted for inflation, which shows its purchasing power in today's money.
  • In the chart, blue is your own money and pale blue is interest. Hover over a bar for that year's numbers.
The formulas we use
  • Monthly equivalent rate: i = (1 + r/n)^(n/12) − 1
  • Principal growth: P × (1 + i)^(12 × years)
  • Contributions (end of period): PMT × ((1 + j)^M − 1) / j, where j = (1 + i)^k − 1, k = months between deposits and M = number of deposits. For start-of-period deposits, multiply by (1 + j).
  • Real value: Future Value / (1 + inflation)^years
Converting everything to a monthly rate lets any compounding frequency work with any contribution frequency accurately.
Tips
  • Starting early matters more than starting big.
  • Small, regular contributions add up dramatically over decades.
  • Compare your result against the inflation-adjusted value for a realistic picture.
  • This is an estimate. Real returns vary, and fees and taxes are not included. It is not financial advice.

Compound Interest Calculator: Turn Small Savings Into Serious Growth

A small amount saved today can grow into something far larger than you would expect, thanks to compound interest. Whether you are building an emergency fund, saving for a house deposit, or investing for retirement, knowing exactly how your money will grow helps you plan with confidence.

What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both your original money (the principal) and the interest that has already been added. Each year, your balance grows, so the next year's interest is calculated on a larger amount. Over time this creates a snowball effect.

It works in two directions: it grows savings and investments over years, but it also grows debt when you carry a balance. Understanding compounding is one of the most useful money skills you can learn.

How to Use the Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Enter your initial principal - the amount you are starting with - and pick your currency.
  2. Add the annual interest rate and how many years you plan to save or invest.
  3. Optionally add a regular contribution, choose how often it is added, and say whether it lands at the start or the end of each period.
  4. Choose a compounding frequency (monthly is common and realistic for most accounts).
  5. Add an inflation rate to see what the future amount is worth in today's money.

The result shows your future value, total contributed, the interest earned on top, and the inflation-adjusted value in today's money - plus a year-by-year chart, table and a step-by-step explanation of every formula behind the numbers.

Why Compound Interest Matters for Your Goals

Even modest contributions made consistently beat large one-off deposits over long periods, because every early contribution has more time to compound. That is why financial advisors emphasise starting early. Frequently Asked Questions

How does compound interest work?

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the initial principal and the accumulated interest from previous periods. Because each period adds interest on top of interest, money grows at an accelerating rate over time.

What is the compound interest formula?

The basic formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(n*t). A is future value, P is principal, r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years. Regular contributions are added separately each period.

Is the Troolify compound interest calculator safe to use?

Yes. Every calculation runs locally inside your browser tab. Your numbers are never uploaded, stored, or transmitted to a server, so your financial figures stay completely private.

How often should I compound my interest?

More frequent compounding yields slightly more growth than annual compounding at the same nominal rate, because interest is added to the balance sooner. Real accounts quote a rate with a stated compounding frequency.

Conclusion
