EMI Calculator: Know Your Monthly Loan Payment Before You Borrow

An equated monthly instalment (EMI) is the fixed amount you repay every month on a loan. This EMI calculator turns a principal, an annual interest rate and a tenure into your monthly payment, the total interest you will pay, and a year-by-year amortisation schedule.

How EMI is calculated

For a reducing-balance loan the EMI is P x r x (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and n is the number of monthly instalments. Early instalments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal, which is why the amortisation schedule is useful for seeing how the balance falls.

Reading the total interest

Total interest is the EMI multiplied by the number of months, minus the principal. Stretching the tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest; shortening it does the opposite. The schedule here groups payments by year so you can compare scenarios at a glance.

How to use EMI Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate and tenure in years.
  2. The EMI, total interest and total payment update instantly.
  3. Scroll the schedule to see principal and interest split by year.
  4. Use Copy schedule to export the table for your records.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Use the annual rate as quoted, not the monthly rate.
  • Keep the currency consistent across all three inputs.
  • Small rate differences matter more over long tenures than over short ones.

Key takeaways

  • EMI depends on principal, rate and tenure only.
  • Longer tenure means lower EMI but more interest.
  • The schedule reveals where your money actually goes.

Frequently asked questions

Is this reducing-balance or flat-rate EMI?

It uses the standard reducing-balance method applied by banks.

Can I use any currency?

Yes, the numbers are currency-agnostic; only the display symbol is fixed.

Does it account for prepayments?

No, it assumes a fixed EMI for the whole tenure.

Is my data uploaded?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser.
