Oven Temperature Converter: Fahrenheit, Celsius & Gas Mark in One Place

A recipe written in Fahrenheit, an oven dial marked in gas marks, and a convection oven that runs hot - no wonder so many bakes go wrong at the temperature-setting stage. This oven temperature converter handles every one of those cases in a single glance, in your browser, with no sign-up and no data leaving your device.

How to Use the Oven Temperature Converter

  1. Enter the temperature exactly as your recipe states it.
  2. Choose the unit - Fahrenheit or Celsius.
  3. Select your oven type - conventional or fan. The converter shows the fan and no-fan equivalents so you can move between ovens confidently.
  4. Read the result - the converted temperature, the matching gas mark, and a warning when your value is below the standard gas-mark range.

For example, 200 °C gives 392 °F and gas mark 6, while 350 °F gives 176.7 °C and gas mark 4.

The Conversion Formula

The converter uses the two standard oven formulas: C = (F − 32) × 5/9 and F = C × 9/5 + 32. Gas marks are matched to the well-known UK scale, where gas mark 1 is 275 °F / 140 °C and each mark adds 25 °F.

Because fan ovens push hot air around the cavity, they cook faster than conventional ovens. As a general rule this converter applies the classic adjustments: if your recipe is written for a fan oven, the no-fan equivalent runs about +25 °F / +15 °C hotter; if it is written for a conventional oven, with a fan you can run about −25 °F / −15 °C cooler.

Pair it with the Recipe Servings Scaler when you adjust a dish for more people, and use the Cooking Time Calculator to work out roasting times once the temperature is set.

Standard Temperature Reference

The table on the tool lists the most common baking temperatures. A few landmarks worth remembering: 180 °C / 350 °F is gas mark 4 and the default for most cakes and roasts; 200 °C / 400 °F is gas mark 6 for pastry and pizza; and 220 °C / 425 °F is gas mark 7 for very hot baking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?

Subtract 32 from the Fahrenheit value, multiply by 5 and divide by 9. For example, 350°F becomes (350 - 32) × 5/9 = 176.7°C, the classic baking temperature.

What is the gas mark for 180 Celsius?

180°C is gas mark 4. In the UK gas scale, mark 4 corresponds to 180°C / 350°F, the go-to for roasting and most baked goods.

Do I need to change the temperature for a fan oven?

Yes. Fan ovens circulate hot air and cook faster, so they need a lower temperature than conventional ovens. As a rule of thumb, reduce the temperature by about 15–25°C (around 25 F).
