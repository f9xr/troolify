Cooking Time Calculator: Take the Guesswork Out of Roasting

Nothing kills a big meal quite like carving into a raw centre or waiting an extra hour for a roast that refuses to finish. The most reliable trick is simple: cooking time scales with weight. This cooking time calculator applies the classic minutes-per-kilogram method to chicken, turkey, beef, pork and lamb, and turns it into a plain hours-and-minutes answer.

How to Use the Cooking Time Calculator

  1. Weigh the meat - a kitchen scale is most accurate, checking the label is a good shortcut.
  2. Enter the weight in kilograms or tap a preset chip.
  3. Pick the meat type from the dropdown. Each option carries its own minutes-per-kilogram rate and doneness note.
  4. Read the total time, shown in minutes and in hours-and-minutes, together with the recommended resting time.

For example, a 1.6 kg chicken works out to roughly 1 hour 20 minutes at 180 °C / 350 °F.

The Minutes-per-Kilogram Method

Total time = weight (kg) × minutes per kg. The rates used by this calculator are the ones home cooks and UK cooking resources rely on: chicken and pork around 45–50 min/kg, turkey around 45 min/kg, beef around 40 min/kg for a medium roast, and lamb around 45 min/kg. These assume the oven is preheated to about 180–190 °C.

Check the oven dial first with the Oven Temperature Converter, and when you scale a recipe to more guests, adjust the whole menu with the Recipe Servings Scaler.

Resting and Safe Doneness

Always let meat rest before carving - the release of juices is what keeps a roast moist, and carry-over heat raises the centre another few degrees. Use a thermometer as the final judge: poultry at least 74 °C at the thickest part, pork around 70 °C, and beef or lamb to your preferred doneness. If in doubt, the few seconds a probe takes is far cheaper than a ruined roast.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I roast a chicken per kilogram?

A whole chicken takes about 45 to 50 minutes per kilogram at 180°C / 350°F. A 1.6 kg chicken therefore needs roughly 1 hour 20 minutes, plus 10 to 15 minutes of resting.

How do I know when a roast is safely cooked?

Time-by-weight is a starting estimate, not a guarantee. Always use a meat thermometer: poultry should reach at least 74°C at the thickest part, pork around 70°C, and beef to your preferred doneness.

Do I count resting time in the cooking time?

No. Resting happens after the roast leaves the oven and is not part of the cook time, but it does carry-over-cook the meat a few degrees, so you can pull the roast slightly before the target temperature.

Conclusion

A roast that lands on the table on time and cooked through is one of the most satisfying wins in home cooking. Use the minutes-per-kilogram method, confirm with a thermometer, and let the roast rest - your carving board will thank you.
