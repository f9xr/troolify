Recipe Finder: Find a Meal From a Name or a Single Ingredient

Staring at a full fridge and no plan, or a craving with no recipe? The recipe finder searches TheMealDB’s recipe database by dish name or by a single ingredient, hands back ready-to-cook results, and opens the complete ingredients, method and video for any of them.

How the search works

Type a dish like "lasagne" or an ingredient like "carrot" and hit Search. The tool queries TheMealDB’s public recipe API straight from your browser and lays the matches out as cards, each with a photo and the dish name. Clicking any card opens the full recipe: measured ingredients, step-by-step method, cuisine and category, a YouTube walkthrough when one exists, and the original source when one is credited.

Because the request runs entirely in your tab, there is no account, no saved history and nothing Troolify ever stores - the same zero-retention promise as every other tool here.

Surprise me: the random recipe button

When you do not even know what you want, hit Random. The tool pulls one dish at random from the database - a great way to break a menu rut, try a cuisine you would not have picked, or settle a "you decide" argument. If it is not right, click again; the next pick only takes a second.

Everything you need in the recipe card

The detail view shows the ingredient list with each amount matched to its measure - no more guessing whether a "cup" is of flour or of something else. Below it sits the method, kept on one scrollable page so you can cook straight from the phone that found the recipe. Category and world-area tags give context at a glance, and the video or source links let you follow a filmed version or the author’s original write-up.

Going from found to finished

Once a recipe is open, the kitchen math tools have your back: scale it to the people you are feeding with the recipe servings scaler, convert oven calls between fan and conventional with the oven temperature converter, and estimate roasting time for any meat with the cooking time calculator.

How to use Recipe Finder

  1. Type a dish name or an ingredient into the search box and press Search.
  2. Click a result card to open the full recipe.
  3. Read the measured ingredients and follow the step-by-step method.
  4. Prefer to be surprised? Hit Random for a dish picked on the spot.

Use cases

Search by what is already in the fridge ("chicken", "spinach") and pick a meal to cook now.

2. Meal planning

Plan a week by searching categories of dishes, then scaling the chosen recipes with the servings scaler.

Tips for getting accurate results

  • Search by the main ingredient rather than a full dish name when you want options.
  • Try a generic term like "curry" or "pasta" for a broad set of results.
  • If a recipe came out exactly right, remember its name - then scale it with the recipe servings scaler next time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the recipe finder really free?

Yes. Searching and opening recipes costs nothing, needs no account, and pulls from TheMealDB’s free public API.

Where does the recipe data come from?

The tool queries TheMealDB, a public recipe database. The card shows the cuisine area and, when available, a link to the original source.

Do you store my searches?

No. The search runs in your browser and Troolify retains nothing - no account, no history, no tracking.

Can you search by ingredient?

Yes. Type any single ingredient such as "chicken", "cheese" or "spinach" and the tool returns recipes built around it.

How do I adjust a found recipe for my family size?

Open the recipe to get its ingredient amounts, then paste them into the recipe servings scaler in Cooking Tools to multiply or divide them cleanly.

What if the video or source link is missing?

Not every recipe has one. The details card shows the method either way, and only renders video and source links when TheMealDB provides them.

Conclusion

From a single ingredient to a full dinner plan, the recipe finder turns the "what do we eat?" question into a menu in seconds - then the recipe servings scaler, oven temperature converter and cooking time calculator carry the recipe across the finish line.
